
    What’s your approach to managing money? A few years ago, if someone asked me that, I probably would have shrugged and said, “I’ll think about it tomorrow.” Not smart. No surprise my net worth was low back then.

Without a clear money plan, it’s hard to become wealthy. The average millennial makes about $40,000 to $50,000 per year and may owe $20,000 to $40,000 in student loans.

High-achieving people, however, can earn up to seven times more than average. This difference is huge, and it applies to all age groups.

When I finally got serious about my finances, I didn’t focus on earning more money first. Many people think the solution to financial problems is simply making more money, but that’s a mistake. More money often comes with more problems.

Yes, increasing your income matters for your career and bank account. But the smarter approach is to manage money effectively. A good strategy will set you apart from average earners. Here are five personal finance principles I follow:

-Want Less
It takes far more time to earn money than to spend it. You work thousands of hours to make a certain amount, and then you can spend it quickly on a car, vacation, or gadgets. Yet, we often act like money isn’t limited. The easiest way to grow your wealth is to control your desires. Even ancient Stoics emphasized this—freedom comes from needing less.

-Understand How the Economy Works
Learn the basics: interest rates, inflation, bonds, market cycles, debt, and why governments print money. You don’t have to be an expert, but understanding the system prevents panic when markets fluctuate. A book like A Random Walk Down Wall Street or reliable sites like Investopedia can give you a strong foundation. Knowledge helps you make informed decisions instead of reacting emotionally.

-Avoid Bad Debt
Debt can destroy wealth faster than almost anything else. Borrowing isn’t always bad—it can be smart for business, real estate, or education—but never take on loans for items that lose value, like cars or electronics. For bigger investments, think carefully about the costs and risks. Borrowing money is never free.

-Save Aggressively
Combine low desires and avoiding unnecessary debt with strong savings. Your saving strategy will depend on your situation, location, and goals. In expensive cities, renting may make more sense than buying. Always keep enough cash to seize opportunities when they arise. Decide how much you need in savings to feel secure and act accordingly.

-Plan for the Long-Term
Invest for the long term, not just quick gains. At the same time, manage short-term income to cover bills. Build multiple income streams and improve your skills. The more skills you have, the more earning power you gain. Relying on one source of income is risky—diversifying protects you if one source disappears.

-Start Now-
These rules are not just about earning more. Focus first on managing what you have and improving your skills. The key question is: Can you create value? If not, make it your priority. Learn from books, investors, successful people, and your own experiences. Generating value, whether by working or investing, is the foundation of growing your net worth.

When you learn to grow your bank account rather than let it shrink, you’re already on the path to increasing your wealth.
