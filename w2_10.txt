

  Friday arrived quickly. Maya was nervous all day. She arranged chairs in a circle near the front of the shop, placed a tray of cookies on a small table, and lit a few candles for warmth.

By 5:45, the first guests arrived.

Lina came in wearing a bright red scarf and carrying a tiny notebook.

“This is so cute!” she said, looking around.

Then came Mrs. Bell with a large bag of ginger biscuits. “I haven’t read the book,” she whispered, “but I brought snacks.”

Soon after, a young man named Arjun walked in. He had just moved to town last month and had seen the sign in the window.

“I love small book clubs,” he said. “Big ones feel too much like school.”

Elias was the last to arrive. He looked surprised to see the small crowd.

“Didn’t think it would be this popular,” he whispered to Maya.

“Me neither,” she whispered back.

They started with simple introductions. Each person shared their name, a book they liked, and a fun fact. When it was Elias’s turn, he said:

“I’m Elias. I live above this shop. I’m not great at talking in groups, but I like listening.”

Everyone smiled at that.

They discussed the short novel Maya had picked—an old classic about two strangers on a train. The conversation was warm, filled with laughter and thoughtful pauses.

Maya watched Elias from time to time. He didn’t talk much, but when he did, he chose his words carefully. People listened.

After the group left, Maya and Elias stayed behind to clean up.

“That went well,” Elias said.

“Better than I hoped,” she agreed.

She wiped the table slowly. “You were good in the group.”

He looked a little embarrassed. “I usually stay quiet in crowds.”

“You still said the best thing of the night.”

“Oh? What was that?”

“That stories aren’t just about what happens—they’re about what stays with us afterward.”

He looked down, smiling. “I believe that.”

She moved a little closer. “Elias?”

“Yeah?”

“Are we… becoming something?”

He didn’t answer right away. Instead, he took a deep breath.

“I think we already are.”

Maya’s heart skipped. It wasn’t a big confession. It wasn’t dramatic. But it was real.

They stood in the middle of the quiet shop, just looking at each other. Outside, the street was empty, and the sea breeze moved softly past the windows.

It wasn’t love. Not yet.

But it was something beginning.
