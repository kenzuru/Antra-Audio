

  To build a good habit, you should make it simple and easy. Many people think they need motivation or discipline, but James Clear says that your environment matters more.

If something is easy to do, you’re more likely to do it. If it’s hard, you’ll stop. So change your environment to help your habit. For example:

-Want to eat healthier? Keep fruits on the table.

-Want to read more? Put a book on your pillow.

-Want to exercise? Lay out your workout clothes the night before.

This is called “environment design.” It means arranging your space so that good habits are easy to start and bad habits are harder to do.

Also, try the Two-Minute Rule. Start a new habit by doing it for just 2 minutes. Want to write more? Just write for 2 minutes. Want to meditate? Sit quietly for 2 minutes. Starting is the hardest part—after that, it’s easier to keep going.

Good habits should feel simple. Don’t wait for motivation. Make your new habit so easy, you can’t say no.
