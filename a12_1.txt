The human body has a long genetic message made of about three billion letters. Small differences in the order of these letters make each person different from others. These tiny changes shape how we look and how our body works.

Members of the same family share many of these small differences. Even distant relatives share part of the same pattern. Because of this, scientists use DNA to learn if people are related. Modern genetic testing companies read this code and give people reports about their health and family history. Sometimes the results surprise people and change important life choices.

Many people take DNA tests to check if certain illnesses pass through their family. Others use them to know if they can safely have children. Adopted people often use genetic tests to search for their real parents. Some parents also use them to confirm family relationships.
