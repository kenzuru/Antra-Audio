
 
  Monday morning brought color to the street. Maya opened the bookstore as usual, then stepped outside to water the small flower pots by the door. Across the road, a bright yellow cart was parked near the bakery. It was filled with fresh flowers—roses, tulips, daisies—and behind it stood a cheerful young woman arranging bunches in small vases.

Maya smiled and crossed the street.

“New in town?” she asked.

The girl looked up. She had curly hair tied back in a scarf and a face full of energy. “Hi! I’m Lina. Just moved here last week. I used to run a flower stand in the city, but Seabrook seemed like a better place for quiet mornings and fresh starts.”

Maya introduced herself and bought a small bunch of daisies for the shop.

As she returned to the bookstore, she saw Elias coming down the stairs with a book in hand. He stopped when he saw the flowers.

“Someone has admirers?” he teased.

She rolled her eyes. “A gift from me to the shop. Or maybe from Lina, the new flower cart girl across the street.”

“She seems… energetic.”

“She is. You should say hi.”

“I might. Though I’m not sure I’m a flower guy.”

Maya looked at the book in his hand. “Need a new one already?”

“Almost. I didn’t expect to get back into reading so fast.”

“That’s the danger of working above a bookstore,” she said, smiling.

Later that afternoon, Lina stepped into the store with a gentle knock. She wandered through the shelves, her eyes curious.

“This place is amazing,” she said. “It smells like old stories.”

“That’s the best kind of smell,” Maya replied.

They talked for a while, and Maya learned that Lina was also a painter, mostly watercolor flowers. She lived in a rented room above the café down the street.

“I didn’t expect to make friends so fast here,” Lina said. “People in small towns usually take time.”

Maya smiled. “Maybe it’s the flowers.”

Or maybe, she thought, it was the right time for change—for all of them.
