

  This habit is about self-care. “Sharpen the saw” means to renew yourself—your body, your mind, your heart, and your spirit—so you stay strong and balanced.

Imagine using a saw to cut wood. At first, it works well. But after a long time, it gets dull. If you don’t stop and sharpen it, it will stop working. People are the same. If we never rest, relax, learn new things, or care for our health, we get tired, stressed, and weak. We can’t do our best work.

Covey says you must care for four parts of yourself:

1. Body – Eat healthy food, exercise, drink water, and get enough sleep. If your body feels good, you will have more energy and feel better every day.

2. Mind – Keep learning. Read books, take classes, try new things, and stay curious. A strong mind helps you solve problems and grow smarter.

3. Heart – Build strong relationships. Spend time with family and friends, show kindness, and express your feelings. Love and friendship make you feel happy and safe.

4. Spirit – Do things that give your life meaning. Pray, meditate, walk in nature, or enjoy music. Think about your values and what makes you feel calm and focused inside.

You don’t need to do big things. Even 15–30 minutes a day of reading, walking, or relaxing helps. This habit reminds us that success is not only about working hard. It’s also about resting and renewing.

By sharpening your saw often, you avoid stress, feel happier, and can keep helping others too. A strong person helps build a strong life.
