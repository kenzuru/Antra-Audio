

 The bell above the bookstore door chimed as Maya stepped inside the next morning. The smell of old books and warm wood was familiar, comforting.

Elias was already there. He stood near the fiction section, pretending to read. When he saw her, he set the book down.

“I wasn’t sure you’d come,” he said.

“I almost didn’t.”

A beat passed in silence.

Maya took a deep breath. “Last night, I kept asking myself—what am I afraid of?”

“And?”

“I’m afraid of loving someone who might leave again. But I’m more afraid of not loving at all.”

Elias stepped forward, slowly. “I’m not perfect, Maya. I make mistakes. I get scared. But I’m done running. I want to stay. With you.”

Tears welled up in her eyes. “Then stay.”

He reached for her hand. “Are you sure?”

She smiled. “I think I’ve been sure for a long time.”

They stood like that for a moment, hand in hand, surrounded by stories of a thousand other lives. Outside, spring flowers bloomed under the bookstore windows.

Just then, Nico walked in.

He stopped when he saw them.

Maya turned. “Nico—”

He held up a hand. “It’s okay. I knew, even before you did.”

She stepped forward. “You’ve been a good friend. I never meant to hurt you.”

Nico gave her a kind smile. “You didn’t. You helped me grow. I guess we all helped each other.”

Elias offered his hand. “Thank you… for looking after her.”

Nico shook it. “Don’t screw it up.”
