

 They walked together through the market, past glowing lanterns and the sound of laughter. But silence stretched between them.

“I liked your story,” Elias said.

“Thanks.”

They sat on a bench near the bookstore. Maya’s hands curled around the warm cup.

“What’s wrong?” he asked gently.

“I don’t want to be your escape,” she said. “You left without saying goodbye, Elias.”

“I didn’t know what I was feeling.”

“That’s not an excuse.”

He nodded. “You’re right.”

Maya looked up. “You hurt me. And then you just came back like it was okay.”

“I came back to make it okay.”

A long pause.

Then Maya said quietly, “Nico’s back.”

Elias blinked. “The guy you mentioned?”

“He’s a friend. And… I don’t know. He listens. He doesn’t run.”

The air shifted.

Elias stood. “Maybe I should go.”

“Elias—”

“No. I get it. I just… I thought we had something real.”

Maya stood too. “We did. We do. But I’m scared.”

“Of what?”

“Of trusting someone again. Of giving my heart and being left behind.”

Elias stepped closer. “Then let me prove I’m not going anywhere.”

Maya didn’t reply. The wind brushed past them, soft and cool.

“I’ll be at the bookstore tomorrow,” he said. “If you want to talk.”

Then he walked away, leaving Maya staring at the stars.

She felt the pull of two choices—safe and familiar Nico, or Elias, who had shaken her world.

And tomorrow, she’d have to choose.
