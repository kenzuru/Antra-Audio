

  Jake’s mind raced as he stared at the old wooden stairs leading down to Uncle Ben’s basement.

“All right,” Uncle Ben said, unlocking the door. “If you want answers, some of the truth lies here.”

The basement smelled of dust and old wood. Boxes and old furniture were stacked in corners. A single bare lightbulb hung from the ceiling.

Ben pulled out a large, leather-bound book from a crate. The cover was cracked and worn.

“This is the town’s history — the real one,” he said. “Not what you read in the library.”

Jake sat on an old chair, his eyes wide.

Ben opened the book and showed a page with a faded drawing — a figure surrounded by trees, dark and menacing.

“This is the Shadow Man,” Ben said quietly. “People believe he’s a spirit tied to the forest, hungry for souls.”

“Why does he take kids?” Jake asked.

“Long ago,” Ben began, “there was a terrible fire in the woods. A man tried to stop it but failed. He died, and his spirit became trapped between worlds. Over time, the Shadow Man grew stronger, feeding on fear and despair.”

Jake swallowed hard. “Can he be stopped?”

Ben shook his head. “No one knows. The town has tried rituals, prayers, anything to keep him away. But every ten years, he takes another child.”

Elena and Derek came down the stairs, their faces serious.

Elena held a small device she had built — a scanner to read the symbols from the notebook.

“It picked up something strange in the basement,” she said, showing the screen.

Jake looked around and noticed scratches on the wooden floor near the back wall.

“Maybe there’s a secret here,” Derek said, crouching down.

Jake pushed aside a loose board. Behind it, a small metal box was hidden.

Ben helped open it. Inside were old letters, photographs, and a rusty key.

One photo showed a group of children standing near the forest edge — including a boy who looked just like Lucas.

Jake’s hands shook.

“Who are they?” Jake asked.

Ben sighed. “They’re the lost kids. Those who vanished over the years.”

One letter caught Jake’s eye. It was addressed to “The Guardians of Black Hollow.”

Ben explained, “Long ago, a secret group tried to protect the town from the Shadow Man. They left clues, hoping someone would finish their work.”

Jake looked at the rusty key. “What does this open?”

Ben smiled slightly. “There’s an old cabin deep in the forest. The Guardians used it as a base. If the Shadow Man is real, that cabin might hold the answers.”

Elena nodded. “We have to find it.”

The three friends shared a look.

The hunt was no longer just about Lucas. It was about stopping the Shadow Man — before the next cycle begins.

Suddenly, the basement door slammed shut.

The light flickered.

A cold breeze swept through the room.

And in the darkness, Jake heard a whisper:

“You cannot hide.”
