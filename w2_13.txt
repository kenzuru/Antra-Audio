

  Two days after the storm, the town returned to normal. The streets were sunny again, the ocean calm. But the bookstore was not so quiet anymore.

A new visitor arrived—Claire.

She stepped into the shop on a Thursday afternoon. Tall, sharp-eyed, and dressed in a bright red coat, she looked out of place in the small seaside town.

“Excuse me,” she said to Maya at the counter. “I’m looking for Elias Carter.”

Maya froze. “He’s… upstairs. Do you know him?”

Claire smiled. “Yes. I’m his sister.”

Maya blinked. “Oh. I didn’t know he had one.”

“He doesn’t talk about family much, I’m guessing,” Claire said with a raised eyebrow. “Could you tell him I’m here?”

Maya nodded and ran upstairs. Elias was sitting on the floor, sorting books into boxes.

“Your sister is downstairs.”

He looked up, surprised. “Claire?”

“Yep. In a red coat. Looks serious.”

Elias stood up slowly. “This should be interesting.”

When he came down, Claire walked straight to him and hugged him tightly. Maya watched from the side, feeling unsure. This part of Elias’s life was completely new to her.

“You left everything, Eli,” Claire said. “No calls. No emails. Just vanished.”

“I needed space,” he said. “To think.”

“You didn’t just lose her,” Claire said softly. “We all did.”

Maya stepped away to give them privacy, but Claire noticed her.

“And who’s this?”

“Maya,” Elias said. “She owns the bookstore.”

Claire gave her a careful smile. “Nice to meet you.”

Later, the three of them sat in the reading nook. Claire told stories about Elias as a teenager—how he once tried to bake a cake and burned the kitchen, or how he used to write poems and hide them under his bed.

Elias rolled his eyes. “I knew bringing you here was dangerous.”

Maya laughed. “No, please. Tell me more.”

But something about Claire’s visit made Maya feel strange. Not jealous exactly, but aware—Elias had a whole life before this town, and it was coming back now.
