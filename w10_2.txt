
  
 In the garage, I kept reading. There was more. Shaking, I read the next important part:

… he put his arm around Julia. Soon she asked him to take his arm away. He did it right away, smiling.

The book didn’t say what happened to the arm after he took it off. Maybe it stood up in the corner. Maybe it was thrown away. I don’t care. The meaning was clear and right in front of me.

Here was a race of creatures that could take off parts of their bodies whenever they wanted. Eyes, arms — and maybe more. Without any worry. My knowledge of biology helped me here. Clearly, they were simple living things, made of just one cell, some kind of very basic single-celled creatures. Beings no more advanced than starfish. Starfish can do the same thing, you know.

I read on. And came to this amazing idea, written calmly by the author without any sign of surprise:

… outside the movie theater we split up. Some of us went inside, some went to the cafe for dinner.

This was like binary fission — splitting in two and making two beings. Probably the lower halves went to the cafe, because it was farther, and the upper halves went to the movies. I read more, my hands shaking. I had really found something important here. My mind spun as I read this part:

… I’m afraid there’s no doubt about it. Poor Bibney has lost his head again.

This was followed by:

… and Bob says he has no guts at all.

But Bibney moved around just like anyone else. The next person was just as strange. He was soon described as:

… having no brains at all.
