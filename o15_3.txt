
      Her godmother, who saw her crying sadly, asked her what was wrong.
"I wish I could — I wish I could —"; she could not finish the sentence because she was crying so much.
Her godmother, who was a magical woman, said to her, "You wish you could go to the ball; is that right?"
"Y — yes," cried Cinderella, with a deep sigh.
"Well," said her godmother, "be a good girl, and I will make sure you can go." Then she took her into her room and said to her, "Run into the garden and bring me a pumpkin."
Cinderella went quickly to find the best one she could and brought it to her godmother, still unable to understand how a pumpkin could get her to the ball. Her godmother scooped out all the inside, leaving only the outer skin, and then touched it with her magic wand. The pumpkin instantly became a beautiful golden coach.
She then went to check the mouse-trap, where she found six live mice, and told Cinderella to lift the trapdoor a little. As each mouse came out, she gave it a small tap with her wand, and it was immediately turned into a fine horse. Together they made a beautiful set of six horses of a lovely dapple-grey colour. They still needed a driver, so:
"I will go and see," said Cinderella, "if there is a rat in the rat-trap — we could turn him into a coachman."
"Good idea," replied her godmother. "Go and look."
Cinderella brought the trap to her, and in it there were three large rats. The fairy chose the one with the biggest whiskers and, after touching him with her wand, he became a plump and cheerful coachman with the most impressive facial hair anyone had ever seen. After that, she said to her:
"Go back into the garden, and behind the watering can you will find six lizards. Bring them to me."
As soon as she did, her godmother turned them into six servants, who immediately jumped up behind the coach in smart uniforms decorated with gold and silver, standing perfectly as if they had done it their whole lives. The fairy then said to Cinderella:
"Well, here is a coach and helpers fit to take you to the ball. Are you not pleased with it?"
"Oh! yes," she cried; "but must I go like this, in these old, dirty clothes?"
Her godmother simply touched her with her wand, and at that very moment her clothes were transformed into beautiful fabric of gold and silver, covered in jewels. Then she gave her a pair of glass slippers, the most beautiful in the whole world. Now ready and dressed, she got into the coach. But her godmother reminded her above all else not to stay past midnight, telling her that if she stayed even one moment longer, the coach would turn back into a pumpkin, the horses into mice, the coachman into a rat, the servants into lizards, and her clothes back to what they were before.
