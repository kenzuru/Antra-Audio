

  The wind outside blew gently through the narrow streets of Seabrook, a quiet town near the ocean. Maya sat behind the wooden counter of Harbor Books, sipping her warm tea and flipping through an old romance novel. It was a peaceful afternoon, the kind she loved most.

Maya had worked at the bookstore since she finished school. She loved books, silence, and the smell of paper. The shop wasn’t very busy, but the few customers who came in always left with a smile. Her days were simple, and she liked it that way.

But today, something was different.

There was the sound of footsteps on the stairs behind the store. Those stairs led to the small apartment above the shop. It had been empty for months, ever since Mrs. Lorna moved to the city to live with her daughter. Now someone new was moving in.

The bell on the front door rang. Maya looked up and saw a tall man walk in. He wore a dark coat and carried two heavy suitcases. His hair was messy from the wind, and he looked like he hadn’t shaved in a few days.

“Hi,” he said, his voice calm and a little tired. “I’m Elias. I’m renting the apartment upstairs.”

Maya stood and smiled politely. “Welcome. I’m Maya. I work here—well, I sort of live here too. The bookstore is my second home.”

He gave a small smile, then looked around. “It’s a beautiful place. I like how quiet the street is.”

Maya nodded. “It’s quiet most days. Except when the gulls decide to argue.”

That made Elias laugh softly. “Good to know.”

She noticed his eyes were kind but a little sad. She didn’t ask questions. People had their reasons for coming to small towns like Seabrook. She knew that well enough.

“If you need anything—like help with boxes, or, I don’t know, tea—I’m around,” Maya said.

“Thank you,” he replied. “Actually, tea sounds nice. It’s been a long trip.”

She walked to the back of the counter. “Do you like peppermint or chamomile?”

“Chamomile, please.”

As she prepared the tea, she noticed him looking at the books. He stopped at a shelf filled with poetry.

“You like poems?” she asked.

He turned. “Sometimes. I don’t understand all of them, but... they feel honest.”

“That’s how I feel too,” Maya said, handing him a warm cup. “Some words just stay with you.”

They sat for a few moments in silence, sipping tea as the wind played with the sign outside.

“You’ve lived here long?” he asked.

“Since I was a child. My aunt owns the shop. She lets me take care of it now.”

Elias nodded. “I needed to get away from the city. Too much noise. Too many people.”

Maya didn’t press. She only said, “Well, Seabrook is good for quiet. And good tea.”

He gave another small smile. “Thanks for the welcome, Maya. It means a lot.”

She watched as he went up the stairs, suitcase in hand. There was something heavy in the way he walked, like he was carrying more than just his bags.

When he disappeared upstairs, Maya stood at the door and looked out at the grey sky. The clouds hung low, the smell of salt and rain in the air.

A new tenant meant change. Maybe just a little.

And Maya, who always liked the quiet, suddenly felt curious.
