
  
 There was no doubt about it in the next part. Julia, who I thought was the only normal person, showed that she was also an alien, like the others:

… very clearly, Julia had given her heart to the young man.

The story did not say what happened to her heart after that, but I didn’t really care. It was clear Julia kept living as usual, like all the others in the book. Without a heart, arms, eyes, brain, or internal organs, splitting in two when needed. Without feeling sorry or afraid.

… then she gave him her hand.

I felt sick. That man now had her hand, and also her heart. I shudder to think what he had done with them by then.

… he took her arm.

Not waiting, he started taking her apart by himself. My face turned red with anger. I slammed the book shut and jumped up. But before I could escape, there was one last line about those free-moving body parts that had first made me suspicious:

… her eyes followed him all the way down the road and across the field.

I ran from the garage back inside the warm house, as if those cursed things were chasing me. My wife and children were playing Monopoly in the kitchen. I joined them and played quickly and nervously, my forehead hot, my teeth shaking.

I had enough of this story. I don’t want to hear more about it. Let them come if they want. Let them invade Earth. I don’t want to be involved.

I have absolutely no courage for this.
