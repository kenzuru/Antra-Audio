
    
      
      When he woke up, the room looked very strange. The sun was shining into part of it, and yet the rest was quite dark and shady. So Jack jumped up, got dressed, and went to the window. And what do you think he saw? The beans his mother had thrown out of the window into the garden had grown into a big beanstalk that went up and up and up until it reached the sky. So the man had told the truth after all.

The beanstalk grew up right past Jack's window, so all he had to do was open it and jump onto the beanstalk, which was like a big thick ladder. So Jack climbed and he climbed and he climbed and he climbed and he climbed and he climbed and he climbed until at last he reached the sky. And when he got there he found a long wide road going straight ahead. So he walked along and he walked along and he walked along until he came to a very big tall house, and on the doorstep there was a very big tall woman.

"Good morning, ma'am," said Jack, very politely. "Could you be so kind as to give me some breakfast?" For he had not had anything to eat the night before, and he was very hungry.
"Breakfast, is it you want?" said the very big tall woman. "It's you that will be the breakfast if you don't get away from here. My husband is an ogre, and there is nothing he likes more than boys cooked on toast. You had better move on or he'll be coming soon."
"Oh! Please, ma'am, do give me something to eat. I have had nothing to eat since yesterday morning, really and truly, ma'am," said Jack. "I might as well be cooked as die of hunger."

Well, the ogre's wife was not such a bad person after all. So she took Jack into the kitchen and gave him a piece of bread and cheese and a jug of milk. But Jack had only half finished these when thump! thump! thump! the whole house began to shake with the sound of someone coming.
