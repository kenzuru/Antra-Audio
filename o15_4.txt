
     She promised her godmother that she would leave the ball before midnight, and then she drove away, so happy she could barely sit still. The Prince was told that a great princess whom nobody recognised had arrived, and he ran out to meet her. He took her hand as she stepped out of the coach and led her into the ballroom in front of all the guests. A deep silence fell immediately. The dancing stopped and the violins went quiet, as everyone stared at the incredible beauty of the unknown lady. Then a low murmur spread through the room:
"Oh! How beautiful she is! Oh! How beautiful she is!"

The King himself, old as he was, could not stop watching her, and quietly told the Queen that he had not seen such a beautiful and charming person in a very long time.
All the ladies looked closely at her clothes and hairstyle, planning to have the same made for themselves the next day, if they could find such fine fabric and skilled workers.
The King's son led her to the best seat and then invited her to dance. She danced so gracefully and lightly that everyone admired her even more. A lovely meal was served, but the young Prince did not eat a single bite, as he was too busy looking at her.
She went and sat beside her stepsisters and was very kind to them, sharing the oranges and citrus fruits that the Prince had given her. This surprised the sisters greatly, as they did not recognise her. While Cinderella was sitting with her sisters, she heard the clock strike a quarter to twelve, so she immediately said goodbye to the guests and hurried away as fast as she could.
When she got home she ran to find her godmother and, after thanking her, said she truly wished she could go to the ball again the next day, since the King's son had asked her to return.
While she was happily telling her godmother everything that had happened at the ball, her two sisters knocked at the door, and Cinderella ran to open it.
"How long you have been!" she said, opening her eyes wide, rubbing them, and stretching as if she had just woken up, even though she had not felt the least bit sleepy since they had left.
"If you had been at the ball," said one of her sisters, "you would not have been bored. There was the most wonderful princess there, the most beautiful person anyone had ever seen. She was so kind to us and gave us oranges and citrus fruits."
Cinderella appeared indifferent, but asked them the name of the princess. They told her they did not know it, and that the King's son was very troubled by this and wanted to find out more than anything. Cinderella smiled and replied:
"She must be very beautiful indeed. How lucky you were! Could I not see her? Oh! Dear Miss Charlotte, would you lend me your yellow everyday dress?"
"Of course not!" cried Miss Charlotte. "Let a dirty girl like you wear my clothes! I would have to be out of my mind."
Cinderella had expected exactly that answer and was actually glad to be refused, since she would have been in a very awkward position if her sister had agreed to lend her something she had only asked for as a joke.

The next day the two sisters went to the ball again, and so did Cinderella, this time wearing even more beautiful clothes than before. The King's son stayed by her side the whole time and kept saying kind things to her. She enjoyed herself so much that she completely forgot what her godmother had told her, and so she was shocked to hear the clock strike twelve when she thought it was only eleven. She jumped up and ran away as quickly as a deer. The Prince followed her but could not catch her. She left behind one of her glass slippers, which the Prince picked up very carefully. She arrived home out of breath and in her old dirty clothes, with nothing left of her beautiful outfit except the other glass slipper, which matched the one she had dropped. The guards at the gate were asked:
If they had seen a princess leave.
They replied: They had seen no one leave except a young girl in very plain clothes who looked more like a poor village girl than a princess.
