
  
 Max finally boarded the train back to Central Station, tomato-soaked suit and all.

He sat near the window and sighed. “Alright. No more disasters.”

As the train pulled away from sleepy Greenfield, he tried to clean his suit with tissues from his bag. It didn’t help. He looked like he had wrestled a pizza.

Suddenly, someone sat down across from him with a loud thud.

A girl, maybe his age, dropped her backpack onto the seat. It was bright yellow and had a giant cartoon chicken on it.

She looked at Max. Then looked again. “Whoa. You okay? You look like you lost a food fight.”

Max laughed weakly. “I may have. It’s a long story.”

She grinned. “I love long stories. I’m Maya.”

“Max.”

They shook hands.

“You look like you’re going somewhere important,” Maya said, noticing the dress shoes, the crushed tie in his pocket, and the panic in his eyes.

“I’m the best man at a wedding. And I’m already very late.”

“Oof. Been there.” She leaned back. “I was once a flower girl at my cousin’s wedding. Tripped, fell into the cake. Whole thing collapsed. I was six. They still call me ‘The Cake Destroyer.’”

Max burst out laughing. “Nice to meet a fellow disaster.”

Maya tilted her chicken bag and pulled out a chocolate bar. “Want one?”

He nodded gratefully.

Thirty minutes into the ride, the train slowed down.

Then stopped.

The speaker crackled:
“We apologize for the delay. A cow is on the tracks.”

Max stared at the speaker. Then at Maya. “A cow.”

She nodded, calmly unwrapping another chocolate bar. “This happens more than you’d think.”

Max opened his phone.

Leo: “Where are you NOW?”

Max: “Blocked by a cow.”

Leo: “Tell the cow it’s ruining a wedding.”

Max: “Should I moo in protest?”

Leo: “Max…”

Max: “I’m sorry I asked.”

Twenty minutes passed. The cow was finally removed by a man with a large hat and a bucket of corn.

The train continued.

“I might just make it,” Max whispered, half to himself, half to fate.

Maya looked at him. “You will. And when you do, tell the couple: true love always finds a way… but sometimes it’s covered in tomato sauce.”

Max smiled. “Thanks, Maya.”

When they arrived at Central Station, Maya waved goodbye. “Good luck, Banana Boy!”

He gave her a thumbs-up, ran out of the train car, and practically flew down the platform.

Leo was standing there, arms crossed, eyebrows raised. “You look like lasagna.”

“I smell like it, too.”

They both laughed—finally.

Leo handed him a spare shirt and jacket. “You change. I’ll get us on the next train to the wedding venue.”

Max grinned. “This time, I’ll double-check the platform.”

Leo clapped him on the back. “Good. We’re down to Plan D. That’s ‘Don’t ruin the wedding.’”
