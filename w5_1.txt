
  
 It was 6:45 AM, and Max was already running late.

He flew down the stairs of his apartment building, jacket half-on, phone between his ear and shoulder, and a banana in his mouth.

“Leo! I’m coming! Don’t let that train leave without me!” Max shouted into the phone.

“I’m not the train driver, Max,” his best friend Leo said dryly. “Just get to Central Station before 7:30 or you’ll miss the wedding. You’re the best man, remember?”

Max almost dropped his phone. “Of course I remember! I have the rings. Wait—do I have the rings?”

He stopped mid-run, turned his backpack upside down on the sidewalk, and frantically searched. Two small velvet boxes rolled out and landed near a curious cat.

“Phew,” Max sighed. “Still got them.”

He stuffed the rings back into his bag, waved at the confused cat, and ran toward the bus stop.

The bus was full. Max squeezed into a tight spot near the door. A baby was crying. A woman’s shopping bag was leaking tomato juice onto the floor. Max checked his watch: 7:08 AM.

“Still time,” he whispered to himself.

Then the bus stopped.

And stayed stopped.

People looked around, confused. The driver stood up and announced, “Sorry folks, we’ve got a flat tire. Please wait or find another way.”

Max blinked. “A what?!”

He jumped off the bus, waved down a taxi, and shouted, “Central Station! Fast as you can!”

The driver grinned. “Hold on tight.”

Max’s taxi weaved through the traffic like a video game. Horns honked, people shouted, and Max’s banana (which he had forgotten in his coat pocket) was now smashed into his side.

Just then, his phone buzzed.

Leo: “Where are you?!”

Max: “Almost there. Tell them to wait. Also, I smell like banana.”

Leo: “Not helping!”

When Max finally reached Central Station, it was 7:28. The train doors were still open.

He sprinted across the platform, bag swinging, banana juice dripping, and jumped into the train just as the doors slid shut behind him.

A nearby woman clapped softly. “Nice jump.”

Max bowed dramatically, then collapsed into a seat. “Thank you, thank you. I’m here for a wedding, not the Olympics.”

He looked around.

Leo wasn’t there.

Neither was the rest of the group.

Max’s heart sank. He checked his phone.

Leo: “We’re on Platform 9. Where are you?!”

Max slowly turned his head to the platform outside the window.

It said: Platform 6.

He had taken the wrong train.
