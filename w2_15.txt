

  Maya stood on the platform with Elias. The small train station was quiet, the tracks stretching into the hills. His bag sat at his feet, worn and dusty.

“Sure you don’t want to come?” Elias asked, adjusting his coat.

Maya smiled sadly. “You need time with your family. And they probably don’t want a stranger around right now.”

“You’re not a stranger to me,” he said.

She lowered her eyes. “Still.”

The train’s whistle blew in the distance. Maya felt the seconds ticking louder than the sound.

Claire stood behind them, waiting to board too. “I’ll make sure he behaves,” she said, half-joking.

Maya nodded. “Good luck.”

Elias reached for Maya’s hand. “I’ll call. I’ll text. I’ll write letters if I have to.”

She laughed. “Okay, old man.”

But when he stepped onto the train, something shifted inside her. A space opened—quiet, but aching.

As the train pulled away, Maya waved. Elias waved back from the window, his face partly hidden behind the glass. And then he was gone.

Maya walked home slowly, the sound of the town suddenly too big.
