
  
 The girl cried, but someone stepped forward—her sweetheart.

“I will go and find the golden ball,” he said bravely.

He walked to the place where the ball disappeared. The front gate was locked, so he climbed the fence. Just as he reached the top, an old woman rose up from a ditch beside the path.

She looked straight into his eyes and said, “You may get the ball, but only if you spend three nights in that house.”

“I will,” the boy replied.

He entered the house. It was quiet and dark inside. He looked in every room and corner, but he could not find the ball.

As night came, the house became cold. Strange sounds began outside. He went to the window and saw shadows—many of them—moving in the yard. Ghosts.

His heart beat faster. Then he heard slow, heavy steps on the stairs. The door creaked. He quickly hid behind it.

A giant walked in. He was five times taller than a man, with long arms and huge feet. The boy stayed quiet.

The giant walked to the window and leaned on it, looking outside.

Suddenly, the boy ran out and hit the giant with a sword he had hidden in his belt. The blade was sharp. It cut the giant into two parts!

The top part of the giant fell outside through the window. The bottom part stayed standing at the window.

Then the ghosts in the yard cried out, “There comes half our master! Bring the other half!”

The boy said, “You legs can’t do anything by yourselves. Go find your head.” And he kicked the bottom part out of the window.

The yard became silent.
