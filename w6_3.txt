

 By Tuesday, the whole school knows me as “Math Guy.”

Somehow — and I blame Mr. Wilson — word spreads like wildfire. I walk down the hallway and hear whispers:

“That’s the guy who wrote a love letter to math…”

“He said he stares at math in class.”

“Mr. Wilson called him inspirational!”

And Mr. Wilson? He’s on a mission.

He gives me extra worksheets. Like, not punishment worksheets. Special ones. “Challenge Problems,” he calls them. “For gifted minds like yours, Jake.”

He even printed a gold star and taped it to my locker with a note:

Keep solving, math hero!
— Mr. W

I’m dying inside.

Worse than that? Emily.

She barely talks to me now.

She doesn’t wait for me at lunch. She doesn’t sit next to me in class. No little jokes. No smiles. Just polite, distant nods.

I try to talk to her in the hallway. “Hey, Em—”

She cuts me off. “I didn’t know you were so into math. That’s cool, I guess.”

I want to scream, “I'M NOT!”

But the lie has gone too far.

How do I say: “That message was actually meant for you — it was about how I stare at you, not equations!”?

How do I fix this without sounding like a total creep?

That night, I pace around my room, tossing a stress ball against the wall. Over and over.

Thud.
Thud.
Thud.

I could send her the message now. Explain everything. Just… tell her the truth.

But then I imagine her face when she finds out I’ve been lying about it for two whole days. When she finds out Mr. Wilson’s calling me a genius because I accidentally hit the wrong name.

Thud.

Maybe this isn’t fixable.

Or maybe… maybe I need a new plan.

The next morning, I walk into school with one goal:

End the math lie.

But Mr. Wilson is already at the door, holding a huge white envelope.

“Jake!” he grins. “Exciting news! I’ve nominated you for the State Math Camp! A whole week with the best young math minds in the region!”

I blink.

“Math… camp?”

He nods, beaming. “All because of that beautiful message. You reminded me why I love teaching.”

I fake a smile. “Wow. That’s… something.”

Inside, I’m panicking again.

I never sent the text to Emily.
She thinks I’m ignoring her.
My teacher thinks I’m a math prodigy.
And now I’m going to MATH CAMP?

This is worse than a nightmare. This is math-themed hell.
