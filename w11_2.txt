

 The forest felt different now.

Lena, Tom, and Mia stood close together, looking around. Where the small path had been, there were now only trees—tall, dark, and close together. The forest felt like it had changed behind them.

“Maybe we’re just confused,” Tom said. He tried to sound calm, but his voice was shaking a little.

“No,” Lena said. “We walked in a straight line. We didn’t turn. The path should be right there.”

Mia looked around nervously. “What if the forest is… moving?”

Tom laughed quickly, but it didn’t sound real. “Trees don’t move, Mia.”

But Lena felt the same strange feeling. It was as if the forest was watching them, listening to them. The air was colder than before, and the silence felt heavy, like something was holding its breath.

Whhhhsshhh…

Another whisper.

It was soft but clear. It didn’t come from one place—it was all around them.

Lena’s heart beat faster. “I heard it again,” she said.

Tom nodded slowly. “Me too.”

“What did it say?” Mia whispered.

“I don’t know,” Lena said. “It’s not loud. Just… soft voices, like they’re behind the trees.”

Tom took out his flashlight. It flickered for a moment before turning on. He waved it around, but it only made the shadows look bigger. “Let’s walk in one direction,” he said. “If we keep walking straight, we’ll get out.”

They agreed and started walking again.

But now, every step felt heavier.

The ground was soft and uneven. Roots twisted under their feet. The trees were packed so close, it was hard to walk between them. Branches scratched their arms and faces. And the whispers—oh, the whispers—kept coming.

Sometimes they sounded like words. Sometimes just air. But they were always there.

“Go back… leave… not yours…”

Mia started to cry. “I want to go home,” she said quietly. “I don’t like this. This forest is wrong.”

Lena put an arm around her. “We’ll be okay. Just stay close.”

After walking for what felt like an hour, they saw something ahead.

A light.

Not a bright light, but a soft yellow glow.

“Is that a house?” Tom asked, squinting through the trees.

“It looks like one,” Lena said. “Maybe someone lives there!”

Mia wiped her eyes. “I don’t care who lives there. I just want out of this forest.”

They moved quickly toward the light. As they got closer, they saw it was a small wooden cabin. It was old, with broken windows and a door that hung open just a little. The glow came from inside—maybe a lamp?

“Should we go in?” Tom asked.

Lena looked back at the trees. The forest behind them looked darker than before. And the whispers were getting louder.

“Not safe… not welcome… leave…”

Lena nodded. “We don’t have a choice.”

Together, they stepped inside the old house.

The door creaked shut behind them.
