

  The days after their battle in the tunnel felt like an endless nightmare. Lucas was still missing, and the fear in Black Hollow grew heavier with every sunset.

Jake sat on the porch of Uncle Ben’s house, staring into the dark forest. The wind whispered through the trees, as if the woods themselves were alive — watching, waiting.

Suddenly, from the edge of the forest, Jake heard a faint sound — a weak voice calling his name.

“Jake… Jake…”

He stood quickly, heart pounding.

There, emerging slowly from the shadows, was Lucas.

His clothes were torn and dirty. His face was pale and covered in scratches, but his eyes were alive — full of relief.

Jake ran toward him. “Lucas! You’re back!”

Lucas smiled faintly, but his body shook with exhaustion. “I escaped…”

Jake helped him to the porch and sat him down. Elena and Derek came outside, eyes wide with shock.

Lucas looked around nervously, as if expecting something to come out of the dark.

“I didn’t think I’d get out,” he said quietly. “The Shadow Man... he trapped me in a place beneath the forest — a dark, cold pit.”

“What happened?” Jake asked, leaning closer.

Lucas swallowed hard. “It was like a prison made of shadows and cold stone. I could hear voices — others like me, lost in the dark.”

Elena’s face went pale. “Others? There are more?”

Lucas nodded slowly. “Yes. I heard them crying for help. Some of them were kids who disappeared years ago.”

Derek’s jaw tightened. “We have to find them. We can’t leave them there.”

Lucas looked at Jake, eyes pleading. “I found a way out, but the Shadow Man watches everything. You have to be careful.”

Jake’s mind raced. The Shadow Man’s power was bigger than they thought.

“We need a plan,” Elena said, pulling out her notebook and laptop.

They sat together, whispering late into the night, mapping the forest, marking the places where the shadows were strongest.

Lucas described the pit’s location — a hidden cave near the old ruins on the other side of the forest.

“We have to go there,” Jake said firmly. “We can’t wait for the police. They don’t understand.”

Uncle Ben appeared behind them quietly. “If you go, you must be prepared. The Shadow Man is dangerous, and the forest will test you.”

Jake looked at his uncle, then at his friends.

“We’re ready,” Jake said.

The next morning, they gathered supplies — ropes, flashlights, food, and the silver dagger.

As they entered the forest, the sun was just rising, casting long shadows between the trees.

The forest was quiet, but the air was heavy, thick with danger.

Hours later, they reached the old ruins Lucas had spoken about. Broken stone walls and overgrown vines hid the entrance to a dark cave.

Jake’s heart pounded.

They switched on their flashlights and stepped inside.

The cave was cold and smelled of earth and damp stone.

The walls were covered in strange symbols glowing faintly in the dark.

They moved carefully, listening for any sound.

Suddenly, a distant cry echoed through the tunnels.

Lucas’s face lit up. “That’s them!”

They followed the sound deeper, hearts pounding with hope and fear.

But the Shadow Man was waiting.

A cold wind blew through the cave, and the walls seemed to close in.

The shadows moved, whispering threats.

Jake gripped the dagger tightly.

“Elena, Derek — stay close,” he said.

The fight to save the lost had begun.
