

 The mirror had stopped glowing, but the fear remained.

Lena stared at her reflection, the only one still showing in the glass. Tom and Mia stood beside her, but the mirror did not show them anymore. It was as if they were already gone.

Mia stepped back. “Does this mean… it chose you?”

“I don’t know,” Lena whispered.

Tom looked around the room. “We can’t stay here. We need to leave the cabin.”

“But the fog,” Mia said. “We can’t see anything outside.”

“It doesn’t matter,” Tom said. “This house—it’s not safe anymore. It’s part of the forest. It’s alive.”

Suddenly, the floor creaked under their feet.

Then again.

And again.

It wasn’t just the wood—it was the entire house. The walls were moving, like the cabin was breathing. Slowly in… and slowly out.

A wind blew through the broken windows, but it wasn’t like before. This wind was ice cold and sharp. It circled them like a warning.

Lena’s flashlight flickered again. Then it died.

Darkness.

Mia screamed.

“Use your phone!” Tom shouted.

Lena pulled her phone out quickly. The screen turned on for just a second before turning black.

“No battery,” she said.

Tom tried his. The same thing.

Then the whispers came again.

This time, they were louder.

All around them. Above. Below. Inside the walls.

“The forest remembers. The forest decides. One walks free. Two must stay.”

Suddenly, the front door slammed open.

But outside, there was no fog.

Instead, they saw a clear path through the trees.

A narrow trail lit by moonlight.

It looked like a way out.

But only one person could go.

“Run!” Tom said.

“No!” Lena grabbed his arm. “We can’t leave each other!”

“It’s what it wants,” Mia said, breathing hard. “It wants us to choose.”

Then the back door opened too.

But the view behind that door was not a path.

It was complete darkness.

And in that darkness… something was coming.

Footsteps.

Slow. Heavy. Wet.

Thump… thump… thump…

“Something’s coming,” Mia cried. “It’s coming to take us.”

Lena looked at her friends. Her heart was racing.

Tom stepped back from the front door. “Go, Lena. You’re the one it picked.”

“No!” she yelled. “We go together!”

“You saw the mirror,” he said. “Only you had a reflection. It has to be you.”

Lena’s eyes filled with tears. “I’m not leaving you both here.”

The footsteps were getting louder.

Closer.

And now they saw it.

A tall, thin figure.

With no face.

And long arms that reached the floor.

It stood in the back door.

Waiting.

Mia backed away. “Please… go, Lena. If one of us can escape this place… it should be you.”

The figure moved one step forward.

Then another.

Time was running out.

The forest had opened the door.

But for how long?
