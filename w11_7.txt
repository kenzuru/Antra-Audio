

 The mirror stood tall in the back of the room, its surface glowing softly. Lena, Tom, and Mia stood in front of it, not speaking. The words scratched into the glass repeated in their minds:

“Face your fear. Or stay forever.”

Lena felt her hands shaking. She didn’t want to look. But the mirror seemed to pull her in, like it was calling her name.

Then the glow changed.

The mirror showed something.

Not a reflection.

A memory.

But not just any memory—her worst memory.

She saw her younger brother, Sam. He was only nine years old. They were playing near a lake years ago. She remembered turning away for just one second… and when she turned back, he was gone. Pulled under the water. She never saw him again.

“No,” Lena whispered. “Not this.”

She backed away, but the mirror followed her—its image still clear, still alive. She could hear the sound of water, the scream, the splash. She closed her eyes, but the sound was inside her mind now.

“You have to let it go,” a whisper said in her ear. “Or stay with him. Forever.”

Lena fell to her knees, breathing hard.

Tom stepped in front of the mirror next.

At first, nothing.

Then: fire.

His childhood home, in flames. His parents, screaming inside. He was just a boy when it happened. He escaped—but they didn’t.

The mirror showed their faces—burning, crying for help.

Tom dropped the flashlight. “Stop… please…” he begged.

But the flames in the mirror grew larger.

“You didn’t save them,” the whisper said. “You ran.”

“I was only a child!” he shouted.

The whisper laughed softly. “And now you’re still running.”

Mia stood farthest from the mirror.

She didn’t want to move.

But the mirror changed again.

She saw herself.

Alone.

In a hospital bed.

No family, no friends, no visitors.

No one cared.

She always feared being forgotten. Being alone. The mirror showed her future—a life with no one left. No love. Just silence.

Tears rolled down her face. “Why are you showing me this?”

“Because this is your truth,” the forest whispered. “Fear is your chain.”

Suddenly, the mirror cracked loudly.

The room shook.

Then silence.

The glowing stopped.

The mirror turned back into glass.

But now—it only showed one reflection.

Lena.

Tom and Mia had no reflections at all.

Lena looked at them in horror. “Why me?”

Tom’s face went pale. “Maybe it’s… choosing.”

Mia’s voice trembled. “Only one of us can leave.”

The forest had made its decision.

But who it would take—and who it would let go—was still not clear.

The game wasn’t over yet.

And the worst was still coming.
