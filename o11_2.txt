
    The grandmother lived in the forest, not very far from the village, and just as Little Red-Cap entered the forest, a wolf came up to her. Red-Cap did not know that he was a dangerous creature, and was not afraid of him at all.

'Good day, Little Red-Cap,' said he.

'Thank you kindly, wolf.'

'Where are you going so early, Little Red-Cap?'

'To my grandmother's.'

'What do you have in your apron?'

'Cake and wine; yesterday was baking day, so poor sick grandmother is going to have something nice, to help her get stronger.'

'Where does your grandmother live, Little Red-Cap?'

'A little farther ahead in the forest; her house is under the three big oak trees, the nut trees are just below; you must know it,' replied Little Red-Cap.

The wolf thought to himself: 'What a young and tender creature! What a nice fat bite — she will taste better than the old woman. I must be clever, so I can catch both of them.' So he walked for a short time next to Little Red-Cap, and then he said: 'Look, Little Red-Cap, how beautiful the flowers are around here — why don't you look around? I also think you are not listening to how sweetly the little birds are singing; you walk so seriously as if you were going to school, while everything else out here in the forest is happy.'

Little Red-Cap looked up, and when she saw the sunlight shining here and there through the trees, and pretty flowers growing all around, she thought: 'Maybe I should pick grandmother some fresh flowers; that would make her happy too. It is still early, so I will still arrive there in good time'; and so she left the path and went into the forest to pick flowers. And every time she picked one, she thought she could see an even prettier one farther away, and ran after it, and so she went deeper and deeper into the forest.
