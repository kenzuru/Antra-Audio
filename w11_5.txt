

 The wooden door creaked open, inch by inch. Cold air slipped through the crack like fingers reaching into the room. Lena, Tom, and Mia stood frozen, their eyes locked on the growing shadow behind the door.

Lena held the old notebook tightly in her hand. Her heart beat faster with every sound the door made. Don’t open the door, the final page had said. But now it was too late.

The door opened halfway. The darkness inside the second room was deep—too dark to see anything. Tom raised his flashlight and pointed it into the room.

At first, nothing.

Just a small bed, broken furniture, and dust.

Then something moved.

Something small… but quick.

“What was that?” Mia gasped.

“I—I don’t know,” Tom said. “It ran behind the bed.”

Suddenly, a loud knock—BANG!—came from the front door behind them. All three jumped and turned around. It was as if the forest outside didn’t want them to see what was in the back room.

Then they heard the whisper again. But this time, it was everywhere—in front, behind, above them, inside them.

“She opened it… now you must see…”

Lena turned back to the open door.

Inside the room, the air was shimmering. It looked like the air was made of smoke, slowly spinning in circles. The bed and furniture started to disappear inside the dark fog.

Then they saw it.

Two white eyes.

No face, no body—just floating white eyes in the dark.

Mia screamed. “Close the door!”

Tom ran forward and slammed the door shut.

Silence.

But something scratched on the other side. Slow, long, and deep.

Screeeeech… screeeeech…

Tom held the door closed with all his strength. “It’s trying to come out!”

Lena looked at the notebook again. On the back cover, someone had written a message in red ink. It looked fresh, even though the notebook was old:

“One leaves, two stay. The forest chooses.”

“What does that mean?” Mia asked, crying.

“I think…” Lena said slowly, “I think only one of us is allowed to leave.”

Tom’s hands dropped from the door. “No. That’s not fair. That’s not real.”

But just then, the front door swung open by itself.

Fog rolled in, fast and thick, covering the floor like a white wave. It touched Tom’s feet, and he jumped back.

Then they saw footprints.

Footprints in the fog.

Coming toward them.

Step by step.

As if someone—or something—invisible was walking into the cabin.

Lena pulled Mia back. “We have to hide!”

Tom looked around. “There’s no place to go!”

The back door began to rattle again.

Two dangers. Two doors. No exit.

The cabin was no longer just a place to rest.

It was the center of the forest’s game.

And they were the players.
