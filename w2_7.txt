

  The next morning, Maya walked to the café with her notebook. She often wrote in the mornings—notes, thoughts, little pieces of stories. It helped her clear her mind.

Inside the café, the air was warm with the smell of roasted coffee and sweet cinnamon buns. She found a table by the window and opened her notebook. But before she could start writing, a familiar voice interrupted her thoughts.

“Mind if I join you?”

It was Elias.

She looked up, surprised but not unhappy. “Of course not.”

He sat down with a quiet sigh, holding a cup of black coffee. His hair was slightly messy, and his sweater looked like he had just pulled it on five minutes ago.

“Rough morning?” she asked.

“I didn’t sleep well,” he admitted.

“Still thinking about yesterday?”

He nodded. “A little. I don’t mean to ask, but… is Daniel someone important to you still?”

Maya looked down at her cup. “He was. But that chapter ended. He left. I stayed.”

Elias sipped his coffee. “Do you think he wants to start again?”

“I don’t know,” she said. “But I’m not sure I do.”

They sat in silence for a moment, watching the sky outside. The clouds were moving fast, as if the wind was hurrying them along.

“Sometimes I feel like people come back into your life just to test you,” Elias said quietly.

“Test you how?”

“To see if you’ve healed.”

Maya nodded slowly. “Maybe.”

She smiled at him. “But I don’t feel tested. I feel… steady.”

He looked at her, and his eyes softened.

“Good,” he said. “That makes two of us.”

They finished their drinks, talking about books and silly childhood memories. Elias told her about the time he got stuck in a tree trying to rescue a cat. Maya told him about the summer she tried to build a boat from old milk crates and sank immediately.

By the time they stood up to leave, the clouds had cleared, and the sun peeked out.

“You make the town feel lighter,” he said, walking beside her.

“You do the same for me,” she replied.
