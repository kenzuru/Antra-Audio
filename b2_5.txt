

  This habit is about listening. Most people listen to reply, not to understand. They think about what they want to say next instead of really hearing the other person. But good communication starts with understanding the other side first.

When someone talks to you, don’t interrupt. Don’t give quick advice or say, “I know how you feel.” Instead, ask questions and really try to feel what they feel. Listen with your heart, not just your ears. This is called empathetic listening—it means you try to feel the person’s emotions and see things from their view.

Imagine a friend is sad because they failed a test. Instead of saying, “It’s okay, don’t worry,” you can say, “That must feel really hard. Want to talk more about it?” That kind of listening builds trust.

Only after you listen carefully should you speak. When it’s your turn, speak clearly, kindly, and with confidence. If you understand them first, they will listen better when you speak. This is the secret to solving many problems in school, work, and family.

Covey explains that this habit is very important for strong relationships. Whether you’re talking to a friend, teacher, parent, or coworker, true understanding makes everything better. It helps avoid arguments, makes teamwork easier, and shows respect.

In short: Listen first, speak second. When you truly listen to others, they will trust and respect you more. And when it's your time to speak, they will be ready to listen too.
