

 The cabin was silent again—but the fear stayed.

The fog inside the house slowly began to disappear, like it had only come to scare them. The door in the back stopped shaking. The white eyes were gone. But Lena, Tom, and Mia didn’t feel safe. Not even a little.

Lena sat on the floor, still holding the old notebook.

Tom paced the room, flashlight in hand, his face pale. “There’s something wrong with this place. Something watching us.”

Mia stayed by the window, afraid to look out. “Why does the forest want to trap us? Why only one of us can leave?”

Lena opened the notebook again and flipped through the pages carefully. “Elsie lived here,” she said. “She lost her sister. Then the forest came for her. Maybe… maybe she left something else. A clue.”

Tom walked to the bookshelf and began pulling out old books, one by one. Most were empty notebooks or broken pages. Then something fell from behind the shelf—a second notebook, smaller and thinner than the first.

“Lena!” he said, handing it to her. “This one was hidden.”

Lena opened it. The writing was smaller and harder to read, but still there.

April 2
I hear the voices again. They call my name. They say I opened the forest. I only walked in. Just like the others.

April 4
My sister is gone. I saw her outside the window last night. She was standing in the fog, smiling. But she didn’t have a face.

April 6
The forest says one can go. Only one. I tried to leave. It brought me back. I tried to hide. It found me.

April 8
I know now: the forest feeds on fear. If you fear it, you stay. But if you fight it… maybe you win.

Lena looked up. “Did you hear that? It feeds on fear.”

Mia was shaking. “That’s not helpful! I’m scared all the time!”

Tom sat down. “Wait. If we stop being afraid… will it let us go?”

Lena nodded. “Maybe. That’s why it shows us things we’re scared of. To keep us weak. To keep us here.”

Suddenly, the door to the back room slowly opened again.

But this time… nothing came out.

No whispers. No fog.

Just silence.

Lena stood up slowly. “We have to go in there.”

“What?” Mia said. “Are you crazy?”

“No,” Lena replied. “I think the answer is in that room. That’s where Elsie was. Maybe she left something else.”

Tom picked up the flashlight and nodded. “Let’s go together.”

The three friends stepped slowly into the room. It was cold. Dusty. But quiet.

In the corner of the room was something they hadn’t seen before.

A large mirror.

It was cracked, old, and covered in a dirty sheet. Lena pulled the sheet away.

Words were scratched into the glass.

“Face your fear. Or stay forever.”

Suddenly, the mirror began to glow.

And one by one, the friends saw something in it—something only they could see.

Their worst fear.
