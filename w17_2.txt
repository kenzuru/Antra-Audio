The next morning, Tom carries his boat to the lake.
Other children have motorboats too.
Some boats are big, some are very fast.

Tom puts his boat in the water carefully.
He starts the engine. Vroom! The boat moves fast.
The engine makes a loud sound, and water splashes around.
Tom laughs. “Yes! It works!”

Before the race, Tom tests the boat.
He steers left and right, goes slowly, and then goes faster.
The boat moves well, but suddenly the engine makes a strange noise.
Tom stops. He checks the engine.
He finds a small wire is loose.
He fixes it quickly. “Phew! Now it is ready,” he says.

The race begins.
Boats go left, boats go right.
Water splashes everywhere.
Tom’s boat is small but fast.
He steers carefully around the corners.
Sometimes the boat tilts, but Tom keeps it balanced.

During the race, another big boat comes near Tom.
He feels nervous. “Come on, little boat! You can do it!”
The small boat moves quickly, passes the big boat, and races toward the finish line.

At the end, Tom’s boat finishes the race.
It is not the first, but it works perfectly.
Other children clap for Tom.
Tom smiles proudly. “I built my own racing boat!” he says.

After the race, Tom cleans the boat and fixes small parts.
He paints it again and makes it even stronger.
He dreams about the next race.
“Next time,” he thinks, “my boat will be even faster!”

Tom goes home happy.
He tells his mom and dad, “I built a real racing boat, and it worked on the lake!”
His parents smile. “We are proud of you, Tom. You worked hard and never gave up.”

Tom feels proud.
He looks at the stars and thinks, “I love boats and machines. I want to make more.”
