

  Saturday mornings in Seabrook were usually calm, but today the bookstore had more visitors than usual. Maya was behind the counter, ringing up a few books for Mrs. Bell, a kind old woman who always bought cookbooks but never cooked.

As the bell on the door rang again, Maya glanced up. It was Elias. He had a relaxed smile on his face and carried a bag from the bakery.

“Good morning,” he said.

“Back already?”

“Thought I’d bring breakfast.”

She raised an eyebrow. “For me?”

“For both of us.”

He placed the bag on the counter. The smell of fresh pancakes and warm cinnamon filled the air.

“Peace offering for interrupting your quiet morning,” he added.

Maya laughed. “You didn’t interrupt anything.”

They sat at the back of the shop, plates on their laps, warm pancakes between them.

“I forgot how good food can taste when someone else brings it,” she said, taking a bite.

“I forgot how good it is to share,” he replied.

The shop had gone quiet again. A young couple looked through some travel books near the window, whispering to each other. Outside, the sky was clear, and the sound of waves could be heard even from here.

“You ever thought about leaving?” Elias asked.

Maya shrugged. “Sometimes. I dream about big cities. But when I imagine them, they feel too fast. Too far.”

He nodded. “That’s how I felt after she passed. I didn’t want silence, but I didn’t want noise either. I just wanted… space.”

Maya looked at him. “Is Seabrook giving you that?”

“More than I expected.”

He paused, then added, “Especially here. In this shop.”

She felt a small warmth in her chest. “Well, it’s always been a little magical.”

They finished eating, then cleaned up together. It felt normal, easy—like they had done it many times before.

As he turned to go upstairs, Maya called out, “Wait!”

He stopped and looked at her.

“You don’t have to stay upstairs all the time, you know. If you ever want to sit and read down here… or just be around people, you’re welcome.”

He smiled. “I might take you up on that.”

After he left, Maya stood for a while, staring at the space where he had been. The smell of pancakes still hung in the air. She wasn’t sure what this was becoming, but something gentle was unfolding—like the turning of a page.
