
  
  Max and Leo burst into the church just as the music started playing.

Everyone turned to look.

Max was panting, red-faced, and still smelled slightly of banana and cookies. Leo’s shirt on Max looked a little too big, and the suit pants didn’t match. But the rings were in his pocket, safe and sound.

The bride’s father glared at them.

The bride—Emily—whispered something to her fiancé—Jake—at the altar. Probably something like “Of course Max is late.”

Leo wiped the sweat off his forehead and whispered, “Just act normal.”

Max nodded, straightened his posture, and confidently walked down the aisle to take his spot beside Jake.

Then he tripped on the carpet.

The entire church gasped.

Max fell face-first, caught himself, stood up like nothing happened, and whispered, “Classic entrance.”

Jake whispered, “You okay?”

Max nodded. “Lost a ring. Found it. Got chased by a cow. Sat next to a girl with a chicken bag. Power outage. Cookies. Let’s do this.”

Jake blinked. “...Right.”

The ceremony began.

The music played. The couple exchanged vows. People wiped tears. Max stood still, clutching the rings tightly like they were the last eggs on Earth.

“Rings?” the priest asked.

Max stepped forward, held up the boxes proudly… and opened them.

Inside were two beautiful gold rings.

“Perfect,” Jake whispered.

Max breathed for the first time in ten minutes.

After the ceremony, during the reception, Leo walked up with a glass of punch. “Well, we made it. Barely.”

Max was stuffing his face with wedding cake. “You know, this might be the most stressful day of my life.”

“Same,” Leo said, raising his glass.

Jake walked over and clapped Max on the back. “Thanks for making it, man. And thanks for not actually ruining the wedding.”

“Hey,” Max grinned. “I only almost ruined it. That’s growth.”

Then it was time for the speech.

Leo took the microphone and spoke confidently.

“Love,” he began, “is not a smooth journey. It’s like a train ride with delays, cows, tomato juice, and missed platforms... but if you keep going, if you don’t give up, you eventually arrive where you’re meant to be.”

Everyone chuckled.

Max looked touched. “Did you just write my autobiography?”

Leo smiled. “This story was sponsored by chaos… and friendship.”

Later, while Max was on the dance floor (doing something that might’ve been dancing or a slow-motion fall), someone tapped him on the shoulder.

He turned around.

It was Maya — chicken backpack and all.

“You made it!” she said.

Max nearly dropped his cake. “Wait—what are you doing here?”

“I’m the bride’s cousin,” Maya said with a smirk. “Remember? The Cake Destroyer?”

Max stared at her. “That’s you?!”

“Guilty.” She offered her hand. “Dance?”

Max smiled. “Only if I don’t trip again.”

“No promises,” Maya said. “This time, I brought emergency cookies.”

And as the music played and everyone danced, Max finally relaxed.

He didn’t ruin the wedding.

He didn’t lose the rings.

He survived cows, delays, tomato sauce, and vending machines.

And maybe—just maybe—he’d made a new friend, too.
