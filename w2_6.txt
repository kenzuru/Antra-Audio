

  The bookstore bell rang sharply around noon on Wednesday. Maya turned from the shelf she was organizing and froze.

At the door stood a young man she hadn’t seen in over a year.

“Daniel?” she asked.

He gave a shy grin. “Hi, Maya.”

Daniel had been her friend—and more than that, once. They had dated for two years before he moved away for work. The goodbye had been soft, full of promises that faded too easily.

“What are you doing here?” she asked, still surprised.

“I’m visiting my aunt. She told me you were still running the shop… I thought I’d drop in.”

She didn’t know what to say. Part of her wanted to hug him. Another part wanted to ask why he hadn’t written.

“You look good,” he said.

“Thanks,” she replied. “So do you.”

Elias came down the stairs just then, holding two cups of tea. He stopped when he saw Daniel.

“Oh. I didn’t realize you had company,” he said.

Maya quickly introduced them. “Daniel, this is Elias, my upstairs tenant. Elias, Daniel is… an old friend.”

Elias gave a small nod, setting the tea down. “Nice to meet you.”

“You too,” Daniel said.

There was a pause, the kind that fills the air with questions.

“I’ll leave you two to catch up,” Elias said, and turned to go.

Maya felt a twist in her chest but didn’t stop him.

Daniel walked slowly through the store, touching the shelves. “This place hasn’t changed.”

“No. But I have,” she said gently.

He looked at her. “I came back because I missed it all. The town. You.”

She bit her lip. “That’s kind of late to say, Daniel.”

“I know. I was stupid. But maybe we can talk. Just talk.”

Maya wasn’t sure what she felt. She remembered their laughs, their walks, their shared dreams. But she also remembered the silence that followed after he left.

“Maybe,” she said. “But not today.”

Daniel nodded slowly. “Okay. I’ll be around for a while.”

After he left, Maya returned to the counter, heart racing. A few minutes later, she walked up the stairs and gently knocked on Elias’s door.

He opened it, surprised.

“Hey,” she said. “Thanks for the tea. Sorry about earlier.”

“It’s fine,” Elias said, though he didn’t sound sure.

“I just… didn’t expect him.”

“An ex?”

“Yeah.”

He didn’t ask more. Instead, he opened the door wider. “Want to sit for a bit?”

She did.

And in that quiet room above the shop, Maya realized her heart was full of stories that hadn’t been finished—but maybe, just maybe, she was ready to write new ones.
