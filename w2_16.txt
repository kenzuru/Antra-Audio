

  Three days passed. No messages.

Maya kept busy at the bookstore, but it felt emptier. Even Lina noticed.

“He left and took your smile with him,” she said as she restocked the flower fridge.

Maya rolled her eyes. “I’m fine.”

But she wasn’t. She missed his jokes, his quiet help around the shop, the way he listened like she mattered.

To distract herself, she signed up for the Spring Market Reading Night. It was an open mic event where locals could read short stories or poems. She had never done anything like that before.

“You’re going to read something?” Lina asked, surprised.

“I’m thinking about it,” Maya said, biting her lip.

That night, she stayed late in the store, writing a piece called “The Man Upstairs.” It wasn’t a love letter exactly, but it was honest.

Then her phone buzzed.

Elias: “Sorry I’ve been quiet. It’s harder here than I thought.”

She replied fast:
Maya: “Are you okay?”
Elias: “I will be. Things with my mom are tense. But I’m trying.”

She stared at the screen. It didn’t say when he was coming back.

Just then, the bell over the bookstore door rang.

It was Nico, Maya’s old friend from high school. He hadn’t been around in years.

“Nico?” she asked, surprised.

“Hey, Maya,” he said, smiling. “Heard you opened your dream shop. Looks good.”

He had grown taller, more confident. His smile was the same, though—friendly and just a little mischievous.

“I just moved back,” he said. “Thought I’d drop by.”

They talked for an hour, laughing about old teachers and silly school plays. When he left, Maya felt lighter.

But later, she opened her phone again. No new message from Elias.

For the first time in days, she didn’t wait by the window.
