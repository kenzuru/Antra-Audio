

  The rain began just after sunset. It wasn’t a storm—just a soft, steady shower tapping on the bookstore windows. Maya lit a candle near the counter. The warm light made the small space feel even cozier. She loved this kind of weather. It was perfect for reading.

She sat on the old green armchair in the corner, her knees tucked under her. A half-read novel rested in her lap. Outside, the streetlamps glowed through the rain, painting golden lines on the wet road. The whole world seemed quiet—except for the sound of someone moving upstairs.

She smiled. The new tenant, Elias, was probably still unpacking.

Just then, the sound of footsteps came down the stairs. She looked up and saw him again—this time wearing a loose grey sweater and jeans. He held a book in one hand.

“Evening,” he said softly.

“Hi,” Maya replied. “Everything okay up there?”

“Yeah. Just trying to find something to read. Thought I’d ask the expert.”

Maya laughed. “You came to the right place.”

Elias looked around the shelves. “There are too many choices. It’s kind of overwhelming.”

“Let me help,” she offered. She stood and walked toward the shelves. “What do you like? Romance? Mystery? Something deep and sad?”

He thought for a moment. “Something quiet. Something real.”

Maya tilted her head. “You’re a serious reader.”

“Not always. Just lately.”

She picked up a small book and handed it to him. “Try this. It’s about a man who walks across the country after losing his wife. It’s sad but... also hopeful.”

He read the back cover and nodded. “Sounds right.”

She looked at him again. His eyes still held that quiet sadness, but there was something gentle about him too. He didn’t talk much, but when he did, it felt real.

“Do you usually read in the evening?” he asked, as he walked toward the armchair opposite hers.

“Almost every night,” she said. “Sometimes I lose track of time. Books are sneaky like that.”

He smiled and sat down. “Mind if I join?”

“Not at all,” she said, surprised at how natural it felt.

For the next few minutes, they both sat in silence, reading. The candlelight flickered, and the sound of the rain became softer. Time slowed down.

Maya glanced up at him once. He looked peaceful. The deep lines on his forehead were softer now.

After a while, she said quietly, “Why Seabrook?”

He looked up, then closed his book slowly. “I needed to leave the city. I couldn’t breathe there anymore.”

She nodded. “I understand. It can feel heavy sometimes.”

“My fiancée and I used to talk about moving to a quiet place,” he said suddenly. “She liked the ocean. Said it made her feel small in a good way.”

Maya wasn’t sure what to say. She didn’t want to ask too much, but she wanted him to know she was listening.

“She passed away last year,” he continued, his voice steady but low. “I tried staying in the same place, doing the same things. But everything felt wrong.”

“I’m really sorry,” she said, softly.

He gave a small nod. “Thanks.”

They sat in silence again. This time, it wasn’t uncomfortable. It felt respectful, like the quiet was doing the talking for them.

After a while, he stood up. “I should get back. Thank you for the book. And for the tea earlier.”

“You’re welcome anytime,” she said, meaning it.

As he walked to the stairs, she called out, “Hey, Elias?”

He turned.

“You’re not alone here.”

He looked at her for a second, and something in his expression softened. “Thank you, Maya.”

Then he disappeared up the stairs.

Maya stayed in her chair, looking out the window. The rain had stopped, but drops still clung to the glass. She picked up her book again, but the words seemed quieter now, as if even they were thinking.

She wasn’t sure what had just started between them. Maybe nothing. Maybe something small and careful.

But she knew one thing:
Some stories didn’t begin with loud voices or sudden kisses.
Some stories started with a shared silence, and two people reading in the same warm light.
