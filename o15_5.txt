
     When the two sisters came back from the ball, Cinderella asked them: Whether they had enjoyed themselves, and whether the beautiful lady had been there again.
They told her: Yes, but that she had left very suddenly when the clock struck twelve, and in such a hurry that she had dropped one of her little glass slippers, the most beautiful in the world. The King's son had picked it up and had done nothing but stare at it for the rest of the evening, and it was clear that he was deeply in love with the beautiful young woman it belonged to.
What they said was completely true, for a few days later the King's son had it announced, with trumpets, that he would marry the woman whose foot fitted the slipper perfectly. The men he sent out began by trying it on all the princesses, then the duchesses and all the ladies of the Court, but without success. It was then brought to the two sisters, who tried with all their might to squeeze their feet into it, but they could not. Cinderella, who was watching and recognised her own slipper, said to them with a smile:
"Let me see if it might fit me."
Her sisters burst out laughing and started to mock her. The man who had been sent to try the slipper looked carefully at Cinderella and, finding her very attractive, said:
That it was only right that she should try, and that he had been told to let everyone have a chance.
He asked Cinderella to sit down, and when he placed the slipper against her foot, it slipped on with ease and fit her perfectly. The shock on her two sisters' faces was enormous, and it became even greater when Cinderella reached into her pocket and pulled out the other slipper and put it on. At that moment her godmother appeared and touched Cinderella's clothes with her wand, making them even more beautiful and magnificent than anything she had worn before.
Her two sisters then realised that she was the beautiful lady they had seen at the ball. They fell at her feet to apologise for everything bad they had done to her. Cinderella helped them up and, hugging them both, said:
That she forgave them with all her heart and asked them to love her always.
She was brought to the young Prince just as she was. He thought she was even more beautiful than before and, a few days later, married her. Cinderella, who was just as good as she was beautiful, gave her two sisters rooms in the palace and that very same day helped them marry two important and wealthy men of the Court.
