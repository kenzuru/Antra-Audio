

  That afternoon, Maya passed by Lina’s flower cart. The colors were brighter than ever—deep red roses, purple irises, and tiny white daisies in little jars.

“Hey, flower queen,” Maya said.

Lina grinned. “Hey, bookstore fairy.”

They both laughed.

“I painted something yesterday,” Lina said. “It was inspired by your shop. Want to see?”

“Of course!”

They walked down the street to Lina’s tiny rented room above the café. The walls were covered with art—soft watercolor paintings of flowers, streets, windows, and now… a bookstore.

Maya gasped. “That’s my window!”

“Yep. And that’s you—right there, reading by the lamp.”

Maya stepped closer. The painting was soft and dreamy, just like her favorite evenings. The books, the candlelight, even the rain outside—it was all there.

“It’s beautiful,” Maya whispered.

“You can have it,” Lina said.

“What? No—I couldn’t.”

“Please. I painted it for you.”

Maya hugged her. “Thank you.”

Just then, there was a knock on Lina’s door. It was Daniel.

“Oh,” he said, looking surprised. “Didn’t know you were here.”

Maya stepped back, a little uncomfortable. “Just visiting.”

“I was wondering if we could talk,” Daniel said. “Just for a minute.”

Lina looked between them and gave Maya a tiny smile. “I’ll go get us some coffee.”

When she was gone, Daniel stepped inside.

“I’m sorry about yesterday,” he said. “I didn’t mean to bring up the past like that.”

“It’s okay,” Maya said. “I just… I’m not sure what you want.”

“I want to know if there’s still a chance,” he said honestly.

Maya looked at the floor. “Daniel… I cared about you a lot. But I don’t think I can go back to who I was when we were together.”

He looked hurt but nodded. “I understand.”

“I’m different now. I’m happy. Or I’m getting there.”

He forced a small smile. “I guess I’m a little too late.”

Maya gave a kind nod. “Maybe just a little.”

He walked to the door, then turned. “Who is he?”

“Who?”

“The guy from the bookshop. Elias.”

Maya didn’t answer right away.

“He’s… a friend. A good one.”

Daniel gave her a sad smile. “Be careful. Sometimes the quiet ones break your heart too.”

Then he was gone.

Lina returned with coffee and a curious look, but Maya didn’t say much.

Later that evening, Maya sat in the bookstore, the painting on the wall behind her. Elias came downstairs with a blanket wrapped around his shoulders.

“Cold up there?” she asked.

“Not anymore,” he said, sitting across from her.

There was a long pause before Maya asked, “Elias… do you believe in second chances?”

He looked at her thoughtfully. “I believe in new chances. Not always second. Sometimes… just different.”

She nodded. “That’s what I was hoping you’d say.”

They didn’t need to say more. The silence said it all.
