Mowgli had grown stronger and smarter. He could run faster than before, climb trees more easily, and move quietly through the jungle. But even with all his skills, he knew one thing: Shere Khan had not forgotten him.

One morning, while Mowgli was practicing hunting with Baloo and the cubs, Bagheera appeared suddenly. His eyes were sharp, and his tail twitched.

“Shere Khan is near,” Bagheera said. “He has been seen close to the river. You must be careful.”

Mowgli froze. His heart beat fast, but he nodded. “I understand. I will stay with the pack.”

The wolves moved quietly through the jungle. Mowgli followed, keeping close to Baloo and Bagheera. The older animals taught him how to watch for the tiger without being seen, how to move silently, and how to listen for the faintest signs of danger.

Hours passed. Nothing happened. Mowgli began to relax slightly, thinking the tiger had moved on. Then a low growl came from nearby.

Shere Khan stepped out from the shadows. His stripes blended with the trees, but Mowgli saw the flash of his eyes. The tiger crouched, muscles tense, ready to strike.

“Man-cub,” Shere Khan said in a deep, threatening voice. “You are growing fast. But you do not belong here. Hand yourself over, and I may spare you.”

Mowgli swallowed. He remembered everything Baloo, Bagheera, and Kaa had taught him. Running blindly would not help. Charging would be foolish. He had to stay calm and think.

“You won’t scare me,” Mowgli said firmly. “I live here. I belong to the jungle. You cannot take me.”

Shere Khan snarled and lunged. The wolves scattered to the sides, keeping the tiger from reaching Mowgli. Baloo growled and swung his paw to block, while Bagheera leaped between them.

Mowgli remembered Kaa’s lessons. He waited for the right moment. As Shere Khan moved, Mowgli grabbed a branch and swung it toward the tiger. He did not hurt Shere Khan, but it startled him enough to step back.

The tiger’s eyes narrowed. He realized Mowgli was clever, not just small and weak. Shere Khan snarled, then paused.

“You will pay for this one day,” he hissed. “I will not forget you.”

With that, Shere Khan slipped back into the shadows, disappearing into the jungle. Mowgli’s heart raced, but he was alive. He had faced the tiger—and survived.

Baloo and Bagheera came to him immediately. “You did well,” Bagheera said. “You stayed calm, thought quickly, and used your brain. That is how you survive.”

Baloo ruffled his hair with a paw. “I’m proud of you, little man. You are learning fast.”

Mowgli nodded, feeling both tired and proud. He had learned something important: courage was not enough. Survival depended on patience, cleverness, and knowing the jungle better than your enemy.

That night, as Mowgli slept with the wolf cubs, he thought about Shere Khan. The tiger would return one day, that much was certain. But Mowgli also knew he was stronger, faster, and smarter than before. He would be ready.

The jungle was dangerous, but now Mowgli understood one thing clearly: he was part of it, and he would not run away.
