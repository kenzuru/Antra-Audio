
 
  The road was long and empty. Trees stood tall on both sides, dark and quiet, like guards hiding something. Jake Carter sat in the back seat of his uncle’s old truck, watching the forest pass by.

He didn’t say much. He didn’t want to be here.

“Almost there,” said Uncle Ben, glancing at him through the mirror. “Black Hollow is small, but you’ll get used to it.”

Jake didn’t answer. His headphones were in, but no music was playing. He just wanted the ride to end.

Jake had gotten into trouble again—school fights, skipping class, and that time with the fire alarm. His mom couldn’t take it anymore. She thought a quiet town would help him “start fresh.” So, here he was, in a place he had never heard of, with an uncle he barely knew.

They drove past a broken sign:
Welcome to Black Hollow – Population 1,473.
Under it, someone had spray-painted:
“Don’t Look Back.”

Jake raised an eyebrow. “Nice welcome,” he muttered.

Uncle Ben didn’t seem to notice. “We’ll get home, unpack, then maybe grab some dinner.”

Jake looked around. The town was quiet. Too quiet. The houses were old, most of them with peeling paint and empty porches. People walked fast and didn’t smile.

At a red light, a boy on a bike rode past. He looked Jake’s age. The boy slowed down, stared at Jake through the window, then rode off without saying a word.

Finally, they reached Uncle Ben’s house. It was a two-story home at the edge of the woods. The forest behind it looked deep and endless.

“Your room’s upstairs,” said Uncle Ben, handing Jake a key. “Bathroom’s down the hall. Dinner’s in an hour.”

Jake climbed the stairs and opened the door. His room was clean but simple—bed, desk, small closet. A box of old toys sat in the corner. He dropped his backpack on the floor and lay down on the bed, staring at the ceiling.

The quiet bothered him.

At night, Jake couldn’t sleep. The wind howled outside. The trees moved like they were alive. Then he heard something—soft footsteps outside his window. He rushed to the window and looked out.

Nothing.

Just the trees.

But then he saw it—something tall and black, standing near the edge of the forest. No face, no features. Just a dark shape.

Jake blinked, and it was gone.

Was it real? Or just his imagination?

He didn’t know.

But he did know one thing now.

Black Hollow wasn’t normal.
