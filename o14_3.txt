
    
      "Goodness me! It's my husband," said the ogre's wife. "What on earth shall I do? Here, come quickly and jump in here." And she pushed Jack into the oven just as the ogre came in.

He was a very big one, for sure. He had three calves hanging by their legs from his belt, and he unhooked them and threw them down on the table and said: "Here, wife, cook me a couple of these for breakfast. Ah, what is this I smell?
Fee-fi-fo-fum,
I smell the blood of an Englishman,
Be he alive, or be he dead,
I'll have his bones to grind my bread."

"Nonsense, dear," said his wife. "You're dreaming. Or maybe you can smell the leftovers from that little boy you liked so much for yesterday's dinner. Go and have a wash and clean yourself up, and by the time you come back your breakfast will be ready for you."
So the ogre went off, and Jack was just about to jump out of the oven and run away when the woman told him not to. "Wait until he is asleep," said she. "He always takes a nap after breakfast."

Well, the ogre had his breakfast, and after that he went to a big chest and took out two bags of gold and sat down counting them until at last his head began to nod and he started to snore so loudly that the whole house shook again.

Then Jack quietly stepped out of the oven, and as he walked past the ogre he took one of the bags of gold under his arm and ran as fast as he could until he reached the beanstalk. He threw down the bag of gold, which of course fell into his mother's garden, and then he climbed down and climbed down until at last he got home. He told his mother everything and showed her the gold and said: "Well, mother, wasn't I right about the beans? They really are magical, you see."

So they lived on the bag of gold for some time, but at last the money ran out and Jack decided to try his luck again at the top of the beanstalk.
