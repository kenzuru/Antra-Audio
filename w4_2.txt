

  The next morning, Jake walked into the kitchen, eyes tired and body heavy. Uncle Ben was reading the newspaper and drinking black coffee.

“Did you sleep well?” Uncle Ben asked.

Jake shook his head. “No. I think I saw something outside last night.”

Uncle Ben looked up. “Probably just a deer. They come close to the house sometimes.”

Jake didn’t believe him, but he didn’t argue. “I’m going to walk around town.”

“Take this,” Uncle Ben said, tossing him a folded map. “Phone signals get weak here, and GPS is terrible.”

Jake left the house and walked into town. The streets were narrow, the buildings old. He passed a bakery, a small grocery store, and a library with dusty windows.

Near the park, he saw the boy from yesterday—the one on the bike.

“Hey!” Jake called.

The boy stopped. “You’re the new kid,” he said.

“Yeah. Jake. You?”

“Lucas.”

Jake looked around. “This town… it’s kind of creepy.”

Lucas smirked. “You haven’t seen anything yet.”

“What do you mean?”

Lucas leaned closer. “People don’t talk about it much, but kids go missing here. Every ten years, like clockwork.”

Jake frowned. “That’s… messed up. The police don’t do anything?”

“They try,” Lucas said, his voice low. “But they never find the bodies. Just footprints… leading into the woods.”

Jake felt cold.

Lucas continued. “They say a ghost takes them. The Shadow Man.”

Jake laughed nervously. “Seriously?”

Lucas nodded. “That’s what the old stories say. My older brother disappeared ten years ago. I was seven.”

Jake didn’t know what to say. “I’m sorry.”

Lucas shrugged. “It’s okay. I’m used to it. But you should be careful. Don’t go near the woods at night.”

Jake thought about the figure he saw the night before. “What does the Shadow Man look like?”

“No one really knows,” Lucas replied. “Tall. Dark. No face. He watches first. Then he takes you.”

Jake went quiet. It matched what he saw.

Later that day, Jake stopped by the library. Inside, it smelled like old paper and dust. He asked the librarian, a woman with silver hair and sharp eyes, “Do you have any books about Black Hollow history?”

She gave him a strange look but pointed to the back shelf.

Jake flipped through an old book. One headline stood out:
“Child Vanishes Near Black Hollow Woods – 1985.”

Another one:
“Ten-Year Mystery Continues – Still No Sign of Missing Teens.”

Jake’s heart pounded. It wasn’t just a story.

He took a photo of the page with his phone and walked out.

At home, Uncle Ben was in the garage fixing something.

“Hey,” Jake said. “What happened to Lucas’s brother?”

Uncle Ben stopped and looked at him, face serious. “Why do you ask?”

“Lucas told me.”

Uncle Ben sighed. “There were searches. People talked. But no answers. His parents moved away two years after. Some people say the forest took him.”

“You believe that?”

Uncle Ben wiped his hands with a towel. “I believe this town holds more secrets than it should.”

Jake went back to his room. He couldn’t stop thinking about the stories. The missing kids. The Shadow Man.

And the thing he saw in the woods.

That night, he stood at the window again.

The forest was still.

But he could feel it watching.
