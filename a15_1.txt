Scientists think they have discovered how people built Egypt’s pyramids over 4,000 years ago.

A research team from the University of North Carolina Wilmington studied ancient land near the River Nile that is now dry. Previously, experts believed the Egyptians used the river to move large stone blocks and other materials to build the pyramids. But the exact location of the river was unknown. This dry river area shows that people could transport stones all year, not only during the flood season. This discovery changes what we know about how the pyramids were built and shows that ancient Egypt was different from what we imagined.

Today, we also understand why pyramids appear in groups. They were built near this river for easier access to water and transportation. The dry river discovery helps explain why some pyramids are closer together than others.

Future research will reveal more details about ancient Egypt’s landscape and how people lived. Scientists hope to learn more about the rivers, cities, and technologies used thousands of years ago. These studies give a better picture of life in one of the world’s oldest civilizations.
