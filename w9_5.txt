
  
 The girl had already been taken to the town of York. She was to be hanged. The crowd stood around the wooden platform. The hangman was ready.

He looked at the girl and said, “Any last words?”

The girl looked out at the people. “Wait!” she shouted. “I think I see my mother coming! Oh mother, have you brought my golden ball and come to set me free?”

But her mother said, “No, I have not brought your ball or come to save you. I have only come to see you hanged.”

The hangman nodded. “Say your prayers.”

“Wait!” the girl cried. “I see my father!”

But he too had not come to save her.

Again and again, the girl shouted, hoping someone would bring the ball—her brother, her sister, her uncle, her cousin. None had it.

The hangman said, “That’s enough. I must do my job.”

But just then, someone pushed through the crowd, holding something high.

It was her sweetheart—with the golden ball in his hand!

“Stop!” the girl shouted. “My love has come! He has brought the golden ball!”

The boy ran to the platform.

“Yes,” he said. “I have the golden ball. I have come to save you. I will not let you die.”

The people cheered. The hangman stepped back.

The boy and girl hugged each other tightly, and he took her home. They lived happily, and the golden ball was never lost again.
