

  This habit is about teamwork. “Synergy” means that when people work well together, they can do more than each person alone. 1 + 1 doesn’t just equal 2 — it can equal 3 or more! That’s the power of synergy.

Everyone is different. People have different ideas, skills, cultures, and ways of thinking. Some people are quiet, others are loud. Some are creative, some are logical. When we respect these differences and work together, we can create better ideas and better results.

Covey says synergy happens when people value differences, communicate well, and trust each other. It’s like building something with many tools. A hammer is useful, but you also need a screwdriver, a ruler, and nails. Every person brings something special.

For example, imagine a school project. One student is good at art, another is good at writing, and another is good at speaking. If they fight about who is best, the project will fail. But if they combine their skills, they can make something amazing. That is synergy.

To create synergy, you need to:

Listen to others' ideas, even if they’re different.

Speak your thoughts clearly, with kindness.

Be open-minded, not stubborn.

Solve problems together instead of alone.

This habit teaches us that working as a team is not about being the same—it’s about using differences to create something better. When people respect and help each other, big things can happen.

Synergy makes families stronger, teams smarter, and communities more united. It turns good results into great ones.
