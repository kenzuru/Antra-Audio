

   James Clear says the best way to build strong habits is to change your identity. Instead of saying “I want to run,” say “I am a runner.”

This small change makes a big difference. When your habit matches who you believe you are, it becomes stronger.

There are three levels of change:

1. Outcomes – What you get (e.g., lose weight).

2. Processes – What you do (e.g., eat healthy).

3. Identity – Who you believe you are (e.g., a healthy person).

Identity is the strongest. If you believe “I’m bad at math,” you won’t study hard. But if you say, “I’m a learner,” you will try more.

To build identity habits:

Ask: “What kind of person do I want to be?”

Then do small actions to prove it. For example, “I want to be a kind person” → “I help someone today.”

Every action is a vote for the person you want to become. So don’t wait to feel like a writer, a student, or a healthy person. Act like it today, and you’ll become it tomorrow.
