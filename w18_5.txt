Baloo and Bagheera didn’t waste any time. As soon as they realized the monkeys had taken Mowgli, they headed straight through the jungle to find Kaa, the large python who lived near the old ruins.

Baloo was worried and kept blaming himself out loud. “I told him to stay away from them. I should’ve stayed closer. I should’ve—”

“Enough,” Bagheera snapped. “We can talk about mistakes later. Right now, the monkeys have him, and we need to get him back before they do something foolish.”

Kaa wasn’t easy to locate. He didn’t stay in one place for long, and he blended into the jungle too well. But Bagheera knew his habits. After a long search, they found him resting on a large branch near the ruins.

Kaa lifted his head when he heard them. “Why the urgency?” he asked slowly.

“The monkeys kidnapped the man-cub,” Bagheera said. “We need your help.”

Kaa blinked once, thinking. “The Bandar-log? They are loud, foolish creatures. Why should I involve myself with them?”

“Because you dislike them as much as we do,” Bagheera replied. “And because they fear you. They won’t listen to wolves or bears, but they run from you.”

Baloo stepped forward. “Please, Kaa. They took the boy far into their territory. We can’t reach him on our own.”

For a moment, Kaa didn’t say anything. His long body shifted slightly, and Baloo held his breath. Then Kaa finally nodded.

“I will help,” he said. “But the monkeys move quickly. We must hurry.”

The three of them traveled together through the jungle, following the trail of broken branches the monkeys left behind. They moved fast, not talking much. All that mattered was getting Mowgli back.

Meanwhile, Mowgli was being carried deeper into monkey territory. The monkeys swung from branch to branch, shouting loudly about how smart they were for capturing him. Mowgli didn’t have a chance to break free. Every time he tried to twist away, two or three more monkeys grabbed him.

After what felt like a long time, they reached a broken-down city hidden in the jungle. The monkeys called it their home, even though it wasn’t really built for them. Stones were cracked, walls were leaning, and vines covered everything. But the monkeys loved it because they could climb all over the ruins.

They finally let Mowgli down in the middle of a large courtyard. Dozens of monkeys surrounded him, chattering nonstop.

“You’ll be our leader!”
“You’ll teach us things!”
“You’re special!”

Mowgli was confused. “I don’t want to be your leader,” he said. “Let me go back to the wolves.”

Some monkeys laughed at him. Others ignored him completely. The Bandar-log never listened to anyone—not even each other.

Back in the jungle, Kaa suddenly stopped. “They’re close,” he hissed. “I smell their scent.”

Baloo sniffed the air too. “Yes. They must be at the old ruins.”

Bagheera nodded. “Then we go quickly and quietly.”

As they approached the ruins, they heard the monkeys shouting from inside. Bagheera climbed silently up the outer walls. Baloo stayed on the ground, ready to break through if needed. Kaa moved straight toward the main entrance without fear.

Inside the courtyard, the monkeys didn’t notice anything unusual at first. They were too busy yelling and showing off in front of Mowgli. Some were arguing about who discovered him first. Others were running in circles.

Then a hush fell.

The monkeys nearest the wall froze. Their eyes widened. One let out a short, sharp squeak.

Kaa had entered the courtyard.

The monkeys panicked instantly. They scattered in every direction, shouting and jumping over each other to get away. Some ran up the walls. Others climbed broken pillars. A few were so scared they simply froze where they stood.

Bagheera leaped down beside Mowgli, and Baloo ran to block any monkey that tried to grab the boy again.

“You’re safe now,” Bagheera said.

Mowgli nodded, relieved but still shaken.

Kaa slid forward slowly, watching the monkeys run. “They won’t bother the boy again—not for a long time,” he said.

Baloo and Bagheera thanked him. Kaa simply replied, “Keep the child close. The jungle is not gentle.”

With the monkeys scattered, the three of them led Mowgli out of the ruins and back toward safer ground.

Mowgli was tired and quiet as they walked. For the first time, he understood that not every animal in the jungle was a friend—and that even a harmless mistake could turn into real danger.
