

  In this part, the book tells us: You are wired to win. This means your mind and body are built to grow, solve problems, and succeed—even if you don’t see it now.

Too often, people tell themselves they are not good enough or that they will fail. This thought stops them from trying. But your mind is powerful. It learns from what you do. If you try and keep at it, you gain strength. If you practice, you improve.

Saying “I am wired to win” is a way to remind yourself of your potential. Every time you try something new, even small, you prove it. You might stumble or fail—but each time you stand up again, your mind learns about courage and growth.

Winning does not mean big trophies or perfect life. It means small progress: waking up early, reading once, moving your body, saying no to something that is not good for you, or trying a new idea.

You are already equipped to handle more than you think. When action matches belief, your actions build confidence. You stop waiting to feel ready. You start acting in a way that shows, “I am wired to win.” And that mindset change sets you free.
