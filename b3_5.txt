

  Just like we can make good habits easy, we can make bad habits harder. James Clear says if we want to stop doing something, we should add friction — make it less easy to do.

For example:

-Want to use your phone less? Keep it in another room.

-Want to eat fewer snacks? Don’t buy them or keep them far away.

-Want to stop wasting time online? Log out or delete the app.

If something takes more effort, your brain will avoid it. This helps break bad habits over time.

Another useful tip is make your good habits satisfying, and bad habits unsatisfying. If you feel good after doing something healthy, you will do it again. You can even reward yourself — like marking a calendar or giving yourself a treat.

Also, try changing your environment. If you’re always distracted in one room, move to a quieter space. The people around you also matter. If your friends eat healthy, you will too. If your friends are lazy, you may become lazy too.

So to stop a bad habit: make it invisible, difficult, and unsatisfying. That way, you’ll focus more on what helps you grow.
