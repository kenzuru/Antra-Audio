

  The following week, the bookstore felt brighter. Maybe it was the new flowers Lina brought every few days, or maybe it was the painting on the wall. But Maya could feel something shifting.

She was stacking books on a shelf when Elias stepped in from outside. His cheeks were a little red from the morning chill, and he carried a paper bag from the bakery.

“Peace offering,” he said with a grin. “Blueberry muffins.”

“You’re getting good at these surprise breakfasts,” she said.

They sat behind the counter with tea and muffins. The bookstore was empty, quiet except for the soft ticking of the clock.

“I was thinking,” Maya said after a few minutes, “maybe I should start something new here. Something that brings people in.”

Elias looked up. “Like what?”

“A book club,” she said. “Just once a week. Nothing big. People could read the same book, talk about it, maybe bring snacks.”

“I like that,” he said. “Do you think people would come?”

“Lina would. And Mrs. Bell, if it’s a short book,” Maya said with a laugh. “Maybe even you.”

He smiled. “I’d come. But only if there’s tea and muffins.”

“Deal.”

That afternoon, she put up a small sign on the door:
“Book Club – Fridays at 6 PM. Bring a book and a biscuit.”

By evening, three people had already signed up.

Elias leaned over the list and said, “Looks like it’s happening.”

Maya felt a little nervous but also excited. It was something new. Something shared.

“I’m glad you encouraged me,” she said.

“You’re the one making things happen,” Elias replied. “I’m just the guy with muffins.”

She laughed, and for a moment, everything felt simple and light.
