

 Lena stood frozen at the open front door. Moonlight poured onto the path outside. It looked peaceful. Safe. A way out.

Behind her, the tall faceless figure stood in the back doorway, blocking the room with its long arms. Its head turned slowly, as if it were staring without eyes. It didn’t speak. It didn’t move. It only waited.

Tom and Mia stood between Lena and the creature.

The forest had made its test clear: Only one may leave.

Lena’s legs were shaking. Her eyes moved between her two best friends.

“I’m not going without you,” she said again. “I can’t.”

“You have to,” Tom said. His voice was soft but firm. “You’re the one it picked. We saw it. You were the only one in the mirror. You have a chance.”

“No!” Lena cried. “You’re just giving up!”

“We’re saving you,” Mia said, tears falling from her eyes. “That’s what friends do.”

The floor creaked again.

The shadow figure took a step forward.

Tom raised the piece of wood again. “It’s getting closer. Lena, please… go. Don’t make this for nothing.”

“I won’t leave you,” she whispered.

The whisper returned. This time it sounded like hundreds of voices speaking at once, like wind and breath mixed together.

“The forest chooses. The forest takes. One escapes. Two remain.”

The lights in the cabin—if they were ever real—flickered once more.

Lena looked outside again. The path was starting to fade.

The trees were closing in.

If she didn’t go now, there would be no more chances.

Mia ran forward and hugged her tightly. “Run, Lena. Run now. Live for all of us.”

Tom nodded. “Don’t let the forest win.”

Lena’s heart broke. She didn’t want to move. She wanted to scream, to fight the forest, to break the rules. But her friends were pushing her forward, holding back their fear with love.

“I’ll come back,” she said. “I’ll find a way. I promise.”

She stepped out the front door.

The cold night air hit her face like ice.

She turned to look back—just for a second.

Tom and Mia stood in the doorway, watching her.

Behind them, the tall faceless shadow raised its arms.

Then—

Slam.

The door closed on its own.

Gone.

The cabin disappeared into the forest.

The trees stood still.

The whispers stopped.

Lena was alone.

The path in front of her was dark, but it was real.

She walked forward, step by step, until the forest behind her became quiet and distant.

But her heart would never forget.

She had survived…

But not without cost.
