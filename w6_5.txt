
  
  It’s Monday morning, and I’ve never been so nervous in my life.

I’ve decided:
Today is the day I tell Mr. Wilson the truth.
No more fake math genius.
No more hiding behind equations I don’t understand.
Just… honesty.

I walk into the classroom like I’m marching to my own doom.

Mr. Wilson beams when he sees me.

“Jake! I hope you’re ready. I submitted your name to the National Young Mathematicians Conference! You’re officially in the running.”

What.

No.

No no no.

“Mr. Wilson, wait—”

But before I can say anything, he claps a hand on my shoulder.

“I also printed your message and framed it. It’s on the wall in the teacher’s lounge. So heartfelt. Really moved a few of the staff to tears.”

I look at him in horror.

“You… framed it?”

He nods proudly. “You're inspiring all of us.”

After class, I finally corner Emily outside.

“Okay. I was going to fix this, but it’s too late. Mr. Wilson thinks I’m the math version of Shakespeare.”

She sighs. “So… you’re not going to tell him?”

“I can’t! There’s a framed copy of my text hanging next to the coffee machine!”

She laughs — for real this time.

“You know, for someone who’s supposed to be smart, you really made this a giant mess.”

I smile awkwardly. “So… are you still mad?”

She shrugs. “I was. But now, honestly? It’s kind of the funniest thing I’ve ever seen.”

I grin. “So... maybe we can start over? No math. Just… you and me.”

Emily nods. “Sure. But if you ever call me ‘inspirational’ like that text again, I’m walking away.”

“Deal.”

The next day, I walk into class ready for anything.

Well… almost anything.

Mr. Wilson rushes over.

“Jake! I just got the email — you’ve been selected for the regional math team! Congratulations!”

I blink. “Oh. Uh… thanks.”

“I signed you up for the Saturday math challenge. Hope you're ready!”

“Saturday?” I glance at Emily, who gives me a you-brought-this-on-yourself look.

That Saturday, I sit at a desk, staring at a paper full of numbers.

I sigh.

Maybe I’m not a math genius.

Maybe I’m not even average.

But hey — I got the girl.

And as for math?

Well…

Let’s just say, I’ll never send another message without checking the name first.

THE END.
