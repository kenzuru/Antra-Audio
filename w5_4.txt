
  
 Max had changed into Leo’s spare shirt and jacket in a station bathroom that smelled like cleaning spray and sadness.

“Okay,” Leo said, checking the departure board. “Next train to Brookhill leaves in ten minutes. That’s where the wedding is. Should take one hour.”

Max clapped his hands. “Perfect. What could go wrong?”

Leo gave him a look.

They got on the right train this time—Platform 9. Max triple-checked.

The seats were clean. The lights were warm. A friendly ticket inspector smiled at them.

“This feels... safe,” Max said slowly, suspicious.

“Don’t jinx it,” Leo replied, flipping through his speech notes.

Ten minutes in, everything was fine.

Twenty minutes in, still fine.

Thirty minutes in—

Boom.

The train jerked hard. Lights flickered. Coffee spilled. Max’s head hit the window. Someone shouted, “Was that thunder or did we hit a dragon?!”

The train slowed. Then stopped. Again.

The speaker crackled:
“We apologize for the delay. There has been a power issue. Technicians are on the way.”

Max groaned loudly.

Leo stared at him. “I told you not to jinx it.”

“I didn’t think the universe was listening!”

They sat in silence for a while. Max nervously practiced handing over the wedding rings.

Leo practiced his speech in a whisper. “Love is a journey... not a straight path, but—Max, are you even listening?”

Max was making balloon animals out of the fingers of his gloves. “Huh? What?”

Leo sighed. “You are a five-year-old.”

“Five-year-olds are never late to weddings,” Max muttered.

Suddenly, the speaker came back on:
“We will continue shortly. Please remain calm. Complimentary cookies will be passed out shortly.”

Max’s eyes widened. “Cookies?”

A train attendant appeared with a cart. “Chocolate chip or oatmeal?”

Max grabbed both. “Bless you.”

He took a bite, then froze.

“Leo... one of the rings is gone.”

Leo choked on his cookie. “WHAT?!”

Max dumped his backpack out right there in the aisle. Pants, socks, notebook, emergency deodorant—but only one velvet box.

“NO, NO, NO—”

Leo looked like he was going to scream. “Do not tell me you lost a wedding ring on a train!”

“Wait, wait,” Max said, digging under the seat.

And there it was—rolling gently beneath an old woman’s cane.

“Got it!”

The woman gave him a thumbs-up. “Careful, honey. Those little things escape faster than a cat at bath time.”

One hour later, the train finally started moving.

Leo checked the time. “If nothing else breaks, we’ll get there ten minutes before the ceremony starts.”

Max leaned back. “Plenty of time.”

Leo narrowed his eyes. “Stop. Talking.”

The train pulled into Brookhill Station at exactly 11:50 AM.

Max and Leo RAN through the exit, flagged a taxi, and shouted, “St. Claire Church! Fast!”

The driver said, “You got it,” and immediately took the longest possible route because, apparently, he had never heard of Google Maps.

They arrived at 11:59.

Max jumped out, threw some money at the driver, grabbed Leo by the arm, and sprinted up the church steps—

—just as the music began.
