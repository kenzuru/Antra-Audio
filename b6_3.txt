

  Rich Dad told Robert something very important: Schools teach people to be good workers, but not how to manage money. Many smart students still struggle with money because they never learn about it.

Financial education means understanding how money works — how to save it, invest it, and grow it. Poor Dad believed in school degrees and working hard for a salary. But Rich Dad believed in learning how to use money wisely and how to become financially free.

Robert learned about assets and liabilities. An asset is something that puts money in your pocket — like a rental house or a small business. A liability takes money out of your pocket — like loans or things you must keep paying for. Rich people focus on buying assets. Poor people often buy liabilities, even if they look like nice things.

For example, buying a big car or house may look good, but if they don’t bring in money, they are not assets. Rich Dad taught Robert to buy things that make money.

Most people think being rich means earning a lot, but that’s not true. What matters more is keeping and growing your money. A person who earns a lot but spends it all is not rich. But someone who buys assets and grows their money can become rich slowly and surely.

The key message is: Don’t just work hard. Learn how money works. Financial education is the first step to becoming rich.
