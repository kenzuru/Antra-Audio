

  This habit is about time and life management. It teaches you to do the most important things first, not just the urgent ones. Many people spend their day doing things that feel busy—like checking messages, watching videos, or helping others with problems—but they forget to work on what really matters to their future.

To put first things first, you must know what is truly important in your life. These things are often not urgent, but they matter a lot—like studying, spending time with family, planning goals, exercising, or working on personal dreams. Urgent things can feel loud and stressful, like phone calls, emails, or problems that others want you to fix. But if you always react to urgent things, you will have no time for important things.

Covey explains that successful people plan their days around their top values and long-term goals. They use their time wisely. They say “no” to things that waste time or don’t help their goals. They focus on things that build their character and help their future.

For example, imagine a student who has a test next week. Watching a movie feels easier today, but studying is more important. If they “put first things first,” they will study now and enjoy fun later. This is called discipline—doing what is right, not what is easy.

This habit teaches you to manage yourself, not just your schedule. When you focus on what truly matters, your life becomes more peaceful, more organized, and more successful. You learn to take control of your time and your future.
