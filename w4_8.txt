

  Jake couldn’t sleep. The images of the cabin and the glowing symbols haunted him. He needed answers — more than what the old notebook or Ben’s stories could give.

The next day, he went back to the town library, hoping to find old records or diaries about the Guardians and the Shadow Man.

The library was quiet, with tall shelves filled with dusty books. The librarian, the silver-haired woman from before, greeted him with a nod.

Jake pulled out his phone and showed her a photo of the glowing symbols.

She looked surprised. “Those symbols… they are part of an ancient language. Few can read them.”

“Do you know who can?” Jake asked.

She hesitated, then whispered, “There’s an old man living on the edge of town — Mr. Harlow. He’s a historian and once studied the forest and its legends. Not many people visit him.”

Jake felt hope.

He thanked the librarian and left.

At dusk, Jake, Elena, and Derek walked to Mr. Harlow’s house. It was small and surrounded by wild gardens.

An old man with sharp eyes and white hair opened the door.

“Jake Carter,” he said with a knowing smile. “I’ve been expecting you.”

Inside, the walls were covered with maps and old drawings.

Mr. Harlow sat by the fire and listened as Jake told him everything — the missing kids, the Shadow Man, the Guardians, and the cabin.

The old man nodded. “The Shadow Man is bound by ancient magic — a curse born from pain and betrayal. The Guardians fought to keep him trapped, but their power faded with time.”

He pulled out a thick book and opened it to a page with a strange circle.

“This is a binding spell,” Mr. Harlow explained. “It can weaken the Shadow Man — but it needs a special dagger and the blood of those tied to the curse.”

Jake looked at Elena and Derek. “We have the dagger. But whose blood?”

Mr. Harlow’s eyes grew dark. “That, my boy, is the hardest question.”

Suddenly, the old man’s eyes flicked to the window.

Outside, the wind howled. The trees bent as if alive.

The Shadow Man was near.

“We don’t have much time,” Mr. Harlow said.

Jake clenched his fists.

The fight had only just begun.
