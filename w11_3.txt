

 The cabin was dark and cold.

Lena stepped inside first. Her flashlight lit up the old wooden floor, covered in dust and dry leaves. The walls were cracked, and cobwebs hung in every corner. It smelled like damp earth and something else—something rotten.

Tom followed her in, holding the door open for Mia, who looked like she was going to cry again.

“I don’t like this,” Mia whispered. “It feels… dead.”

“It’s better than being outside,” Tom said. “We can rest and think.”

Lena moved the flashlight across the room. There was a small table in the middle with one broken chair. A shelf on the wall held some old books. A fireplace sat at the far end, but there was no fire—just cold ash and burnt wood.

“What is this place?” Lena asked.

“Maybe an old hunter’s cabin,” Tom guessed. “It looks empty.”

They slowly moved through the house. It was small, just two rooms. The first room was where they stood, and the second had a closed wooden door.

Tom tried the doorknob. It didn’t move.

“It’s locked,” he said.

“Don’t open it,” Mia whispered quickly. “What if someone—or something—is in there?”

Tom turned away. “It’s probably just another room. Empty like this one.”

Lena’s flashlight flickered again. She hit it with her hand, and it came back on. She looked at the wall next to the locked door—and gasped.

“What is it?” Tom asked, moving beside her.

There was a photo frame on the wall. Inside was a picture of a young girl. She stood in front of this very cabin. But her face—was missing. Not covered. Just… gone. Like someone had wiped it away.

“That’s not right,” Lena said, her voice shaking. “Who takes a photo like this?”

Mia backed away. “Let’s leave. Now. Please.”

Suddenly—

BANG.

They all jumped.

The sound came from the locked room.

BANG. BANG.

Something—or someone—was hitting the door from the other side.

Tom grabbed a piece of wood from the floor. “Maybe it’s an animal,” he said.

But Lena shook her head. “Animals don’t knock like that.”

Then… silence.

No sound at all.

Tom moved toward the door, but Lena stopped him. “No. Don’t. Whatever is in there… it doesn’t want us here.”

Mia stepped back. “Can we leave? Please?”

They turned to go back out the front door, but when Tom opened it—

The forest was gone.

Outside, it was all fog. Thick, white, swirling fog. They couldn’t see more than a few steps ahead.

“This wasn’t here before,” Lena said.

Tom reached his hand into the fog. “It’s cold,” he said. “And… it feels like it’s moving.”

Mia screamed. “We’re trapped!”

“No,” Lena said, trying to stay calm. “We’re not. We just need to think.”

Suddenly, something scratched across the window.

Three long lines. Like claws.

They were no longer just lost in the forest.

They were not alone.
