

  It was a cool Friday afternoon when Lena, Tom, and Mia packed their bags for a weekend trip. They were university friends who needed a break from studying and city life. The plan was simple: drive to the countryside, camp for two nights, and return before Sunday evening. They wanted peace, fresh air, and adventure.

Tom drove his old red car, while Lena sat in the passenger seat holding a map. Mia sat in the back, scrolling through her phone. “No signal already,” she said. “We’re really in the middle of nowhere.”

“That’s the point,” Lena smiled. “No people, no stress.”

The sun was slowly setting when they saw something strange on the side of the road. A small, narrow dirt path led into a thick forest. An old wooden sign stood near the entrance. It was broken and covered in moss, but they could still read the faded words:

“The Whispering Forest – Do Not Enter.”

Tom stopped the car and laughed. “That sounds like something from a horror movie.”

Lena looked at the trees. They were tall and dark, and the forest behind them looked quiet and deep. “I’ve heard stories about this place,” she said softly. “They say people hear voices there.”

Mia leaned forward. “Voices?”

Lena nodded. “Just whispers. Nothing dangerous. Probably just wind and old trees.”

Tom grinned. “Let’s check it out!”

Mia frowned. “I don’t think we should…”

“Come on,” Lena said. “We won’t go far. Just a short walk. Then we set up the tent and eat.”

Finally, Mia agreed, and they parked the car near the edge of the forest. They grabbed their flashlights and walked in. At first, the path was easy to follow. The forest floor was covered in dry leaves, and the tall trees blocked most of the evening light. The deeper they went, the darker it became.

After a few minutes, they stopped. Everything around them was silent. No birds. No bugs. No wind.

“Why is it so quiet?” Mia whispered.

“I don’t know,” Lena said. “Forests are usually full of sounds.”

Suddenly, a cold breeze passed through them, and they heard a sound. It was soft, like someone whispering from far away.

“Turn back…”

They froze.

“Did you hear that?” Lena asked.

“No,” said Tom. “It’s probably the wind again.”

But Mia’s face turned pale. “That wasn’t the wind.”

The three of them looked at each other. The forest seemed darker now, and the trees seemed taller, like they were leaning in closer.

Lena looked at her phone. “Still no signal. Let’s head back.”

But when they turned around, something strange happened. The path behind them wasn’t there anymore. Just more trees.

“Wait… this isn’t right,” Tom said.

They were lost.

And the forest was whispering again.
