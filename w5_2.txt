
   
 Max stared out the window in disbelief.

Platform 6.
Leo had clearly said Platform 9.

“Oh no, no, no,” Max whispered, standing up quickly. The train had already started moving. He ran to the doors and pressed the button.

Nothing happened.

A soft voice came over the speaker:
“Next stop: Greenfield. Estimated time: 45 minutes.”

“Greenfield?! That’s the opposite direction!” Max groaned.

He tried calling Leo.

Leo: “You WHAT?! You’re on the wrong train?!”

Max: “Yes, but wait, it gets better. I smell like banana and I’m heading toward sheep country.”

Leo: “Max, the wedding is at noon. We have three hours. Get off at Greenfield and take the first train back.”

Max slumped into the seat. “Roger that, Captain Wedding.”

Ten minutes later, things got worse.

A little girl walked past Max, stopped, and pointed at his lap. “Excuse me, sir. Your bag is leaking.”

Max looked down.

The tomato juice from the woman’s bag on the bus? Somehow, it had made its way into his backpack. His best man suit was now covered in red sauce.

“Oh come on!” Max cried.

He opened the bag. Inside, the rings were safe, but his shirt looked like it had been attacked by spaghetti.

The old man sitting across from him raised an eyebrow. “Going to a tomato festival?”

Max tried to smile. “Wedding, actually.”

“Ah,” the man nodded. “You’ll be the most flavorful guest there.”

When the train reached Greenfield, Max jumped off. It was quiet. Too quiet. The station had one bench, one sleepy pigeon, and a vending machine that only accepted coins—Max had none.

He walked up to the ticket counter.

“Next train to Central?” he asked.

The ticket lady didn’t even look up. “One hour.”

Max blinked. “One HOUR?”

She nodded. “Also, the ticket machine is broken. You’ll need cash.”

Max checked his wallet: three paperclips, a button, and one very old gum wrapper. Not even close to cash.

Just then, he had an idea.

He looked at the old man from the train, who was now sipping tea at the platform bench.

Max ran over. “Sir! Could I… borrow some money for a ticket?”

The man looked at Max. “Are you still wearing a banana?”

Max nodded.

The man smiled. “That’s worth a story. I’ll give you ten pounds—if you tell me what happened.”

So Max told him everything. The late start, the smashed banana, the wrong platform, the tomato disaster.

The old man laughed so hard he nearly dropped his tea.

“You, my boy, are a walking comedy show. Here,” he handed Max a ten-pound note. “Make sure you make it to that wedding.”

Max bowed again. “Thank you, sir. I promise not to ruin any more fruit on the way.”

As he sat waiting for the return train, Max opened his phone.

Leo: “New plan. We’re switching to Plan C.”

Max: “What was Plan B?”

Leo: “Plan B was you getting on the right train. That’s dead.”

Max: “Fair.”
