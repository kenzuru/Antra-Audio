

  The next morning, the sun returned. It broke through the grey clouds and painted the town in golden light. Maya opened the bookstore early, as she always did, unlocking the front door and breathing in the cool sea air.

She swept the floor, dusted the shelves, and arranged a few new books near the window. Business was slow this time of year, but she didn’t mind. It gave her time to think—and to notice things.

At around ten, Elias came downstairs. He looked more rested than before. He was wearing a navy sweater and held the book she gave him.

“Finished it already?” Maya asked with a grin.

He nodded. “Stayed up too late reading. It was… quiet, like you said. But powerful.”

“I’m glad you liked it.”

He looked out the window. “It’s a beautiful morning. I thought I might take a walk. Any suggestions?”

She pointed down the street. “There’s a path behind the bakery. It leads to the cliffs. Not too far. You’ll see the whole ocean from up there.”

He gave a soft smile. “Would you show me?”

Maya hesitated. She hadn’t planned to leave the shop, but… why not?

“Give me a second,” she said, putting a handwritten “Back Soon” sign in the window.

They walked through the narrow streets, past the little cafés and old stone houses. The town smelled of bread, salt, and wildflowers.

“Seabrook hasn’t changed much over the years,” Maya said. “That’s what I like about it.”

“I can see why,” Elias replied. “It feels like a place that doesn’t rush.”

They reached the path and followed it uphill. The sound of the waves grew louder as the view opened up. Below them, the ocean stretched wide and endless, the sunlight dancing on the surface.

Elias stopped and took a deep breath. “She would’ve loved this.”

Maya stood beside him in silence. She didn’t need to ask who “she” was.

“I used to think healing meant forgetting,” he said. “But maybe it just means learning to carry things differently.”

Maya looked at him. “That makes sense.”

They sat on a wooden bench, watching the sea. A few gulls flew above them, their cries sharp and wild.

“I come here when I’m sad or confused,” Maya said after a while. “Something about the ocean… it listens.”

Elias turned to her. “You’re easy to talk to.”

“I don’t always know what to say,” she admitted.

“Maybe that’s the point,” he said. “You don’t try too hard.”

They sat together for a long time, just watching the waves. Neither of them noticed how quickly the morning passed.
