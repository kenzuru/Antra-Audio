

 The scratch on the window made all three of them freeze.

Tom slowly turned toward the glass. Lena’s flashlight moved with him, lighting the window. But outside, there was nothing—just fog. The scratches were still there, long and deep, cutting across the dusty glass.

“That wasn’t there before,” Lena whispered.

Tom stepped closer and touched the lines with his fingers. “Something really did this… and it wasn’t small.”

Mia was shaking. “I don’t want to be here. Why did we come into this forest? Why didn’t we listen to the sign?”

“We didn’t know,” Lena said. “We thought it was just a story. But now—”

BANG.

Another knock from the locked door behind them. This time louder.

BANG. BANG. BANG.

They all jumped.

Tom lifted the wood in his hand, ready to defend them. “If it tries to come out, I’ll hit it.”

Lena shook her head. “You can’t fight something we don’t understand.”

The door was shaking now. Not much—but just enough to see the knob turning slightly, as if someone on the other side was trying to open it.

Then it stopped.

Silence again.

Mia backed into a corner. “This house is cursed. We need to get out.”

Tom nodded. “We can’t stay here. Maybe the fog is only around the house. Let’s try walking through it.”

Lena looked toward the door. “But what if it’s worse out there? At least we have shelter here.”

Tom and Mia looked at her. No one wanted to stay. But none of them wanted to open the front door either.

Then Lena noticed something on the floor under the shelf.

A small, old notebook.

She picked it up and opened the first page. The handwriting was messy but still readable.

“My name is Elsie. This is my home. But I don’t feel safe anymore.”

Lena kept reading.

“It started with voices. At first, they were far. Then they came closer. I told my sister not to go outside. But she didn’t listen. The forest took her. Now it wants me too.”

Lena flipped to the last page. There was just one sentence:

“If you’re reading this, don’t open the door.”

She looked up at Tom and Mia. “This house belonged to a girl named Elsie. She said her sister disappeared… and now something is coming for her.”

“Do you think she’s still here?” Tom asked.

Lena looked around the room. The air suddenly felt even colder.

“No,” she said. “I think she’s gone.”

Suddenly, the locked door creaked.

Just a little.

Only an inch.

A cold breeze came through the gap, and with it—another whisper.

But this time, it wasn’t soft. It was clear.

“You opened the forest. Now it will open you.”

The three friends stared at the door, frozen in fear.

Then the whisper turned into a growl.

And the door began to open wider.
